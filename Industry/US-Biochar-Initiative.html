US Biochar Initiative (now American Biochar Institute)

North America’s long-running nonprofit hub for biochar education, technical assistance, market development, and standards. The organization founded in 2009 as the U.S. Biochar Initiative (USBI) now operates as the American Biochar Institute (ABI) after a March 2026 rename. This note is the vault’s org home for both the legacy USBI brand and current ABI publications.

Primary URLs: biochar.org/usbi · About ABI · biochar.org home · legacy usbi.biochar-us.org

2. Mission, vision, and positioning

Current ABI framing

  • Mission: “To deliver independent science-based information, technical assistance, and standards on biochar.” (About)
  • Vision: “We envision a world in which biochar is a sustainable material valued by agriculture, industry, and the built environment.” (About)
  • Positioning: Neutral infrastructure — standards, technical assistance, knowledge sharing — so producers can differentiate and buyers can procure with confidence (biochar.org).

ABI’s own values language emphasizes Focus, Trust, Efficacy, Collaboration (About).

Legacy USBI framing (still on legacy site)

  • Mission: Advance production and use of biochar in industrial, agricultural, and infrastructure markets to reduce biomass waste, grow the economy, and mitigate climate change (usbi.biochar-us.org).
  • Vision: Thriving biochar industry supporting resilience and decarbonization while sequestering carbon and building circular systems (usbi.biochar-us.org).

Gray’s rebrand quote stresses durable end-use markets and trusted standards as biochar becomes a circular waste-to-value solution (biochar.org/usbi).

5. Guidance highlights (sourced numbers)

5.1 Application rates (Ag Guidelines 2026)

From https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-for-Agriculture-Applications-2026.pdf:

  • No universal rate — field-by-field to address a specific constraint.
  • Literature-framed field band: 1–10 tons/acre with “consistent positive impacts” in numerous studies.
  • Practical starts: 1 ton/ac ≈ 4 cu yd/ac (lowest effective for soil-organism habitat); 3 ton/ac ≈ 12 cu yd/ac (SOM improvement).
  • Containers / planting holes: 5–25% by volume.
  • Economics: higher rates of “ideal” char often better agronomically but not cost-effective; points farmers to USDA / NRCS assistance.

Cross-walk: Application Rates and Methods · NRCS soil carbon amendment in US ag · USBI Guidance vs Vault Notes.

5.2 Charging / inoculation (Ag Guidelines 2026)

Same PDF:

  • Terms bundled: inoculating / charging / activating / blending / mixing.
  • Purpose: fill surface functional groups with nutrients and microbes; reduce nutrient immobilization and first-year yield drag.
  • Media: compost, manure, compost tea, fertilizer, urine, microbial inoculants.
  • Time: 1–3 weeks “has been recommended” (varies).
  • Links practice to biochar-based fertilizers (BBFs) via pre-/post-pyrolysis nutrient loading.

Cross-walk: Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer · guides farm biochar application · Biochar Nutrients and Fertilizer Chemistry.

5.3 Compost co-processing (Compost 2026 Draft V1)

From https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-Compost-2026-Draft-V1.pdf:

  • Recommended addition: 5–10% by volume at the start of composting.
  • Sheet claims include ~20% faster composting (aeration / microbial activity), odor capture, and higher nutrient retention (N).
  • Caution: >20% or 30% by volume not recommended — can interfere with biodegradation.

Cross-walk: Biochar Compost and Manure Blends · reports Napa CASP co-composting.

5.4 Lab interpretation & standards pointers

From https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-Lab-Report-2026.pdf:

  • No federal product-spec standard; incentive programs (e.g. NRCS CPS 336) and IBI criteria often set practical bars.
  • Other pathways may require state/local rules, stormwater specs, or carbon-removal methodologies (sheet discusses Puro.earth, Verra, and European Biochar Certificate context).
  • Points practitioners to IBI lab lists and IBI Standards v2.1.

IBI Standards: https://biochar-international.org/ · CPS 336: https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf

5.5 NRCS funding (ABI + AFT 2026)

From https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-Funding-2026-final.pdf:

  • Practice: CPS 336 Soil Carbon Amendment; financial assistance via EQIP (and possibly RCPP).
  • Criteria summary includes heating biomass >350 °C under limited oxygen; IBI Certified seal or IBI-criteria lab methods; documentation of origin, production method, moisture, particle size, pH, EC, etc.
  • Example scenario language includes 60% biochar / 40% compost or manure blends and ~$160 payment construction (FY2023 scenario framing in guide — state rates vary).
  • Points to Web Soil Survey “Dynamic Soil Properties Response to Biochar” and PNW Biochar Atlas (http://www.pnwbiochar.org/).

Cross-walk: NRCS soil carbon amendment in US ag · answers CPS 336 planning questions · defines NRCS practice 336.

7. Membership / support model

ABI About emphasizes free / independent science-based resources rather than a dues-gated knowledge wall (About). Legacy USBI messaging historically directed organizations toward directory listings and donations rather than mandatory membership dues (older newsletter framing). For current commercial listing mechanics, check the live directory UX on biochar.org / usbi.biochar-us.org rather than assuming a fixed dues schedule.

# Biochar as Durable CDR

## Core pathway

1. **Photosynthetic capture** — Biomass stores atmospheric CO₂ (~45–50% C dry matter, depending on feedstock).
2. **Thermochemical conversion** — Pyrolysis or gasification (typically >350 °C, limited/no O₂) converts a substantial fraction into an aromatic carbon matrix (biochar); gases/oils must be accounted for in LCA.
3. **Durable storage** — Soil application, burial, or materials (concrete, asphalt, composites) so that a large fraction of organic C does not return to the atmosphere on multi-decadal to millennial timescales.

This is **carbon dioxide removal (CDR)** / **biochar carbon removal (BCR)** when the credited outcome is net atmospheric CO₂ stored in a durable reservoir, after subtracting lifecycle emissions and counterfactual storage.

## CDR vs avoidance

**Avoidance / reduction** claims (displacing fossil fuels with pyrolysis heat; avoiding methane from waste) are a different claim. Most modern biochar *removal* methodologies deliberately credit **durable storage of biochar carbon**, not fossil displacement. Mixing removal and avoidance without clear boundaries is a common greenwashing critique—see [[Risks and Controversies]].

## Why treated as durable

- Higher pyrolysis severity → lower H/C_org (and often O/C_org) → slower mineralization.
- [IPCC 2019](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2019rf/pdf/4_Volume4/19R_V4_Ch02_Ap4_Biochar.pdf) · [[IPCC 2019 Biochar Appendix]] F_perm ≈ 0.65 / 0.80 / 0.89 by temperature class at ~20 °C baseline.
- [[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]] refined with H/C_org + soil temperature. DOI: [10.1021/acs.est.1c02425](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)
- Petrographic approaches (Ro / inertinite) argue millennial persistence for highly condensed fractions ([[Inertinite Permanence Benchmark|inertinite permanence benchmark]]; DOI: [10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409)).

**Important:** Standards do **not** typically credit 100% of biochar C—they apply permanence factors, LCA deductions, leakage, and (in some programs) mandatory offsetting of process emissions.

## Soil / materials pathways

Dominant voluntary-market pathway for ag operators: **soil application** (direct spreading, compost/manure/digestate blends, or bedding/feed routes where allowed). Non-soil pathways (concrete, asphalt) also generate credits under Verra, Puro, Isometric, and CSI with different end-use evidence.

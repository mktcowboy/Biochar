# Inertinite Permanence Benchmark

**Citation:** Sanei H, Rudra A, Przyswitt ZMM, Kousted S, Sindlev MB, Zheng X, Nielsen SB, Petersen HI (2024). Assessing biochar's permanence: An inertinite benchmark. *International Journal of Coal Geology* 281: 104409. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409 (open access)

**What it is:** The petrography paper behind the "inertinite / random reflectance" camp in the permanence debate. It proposes a reflectance threshold for classing biochar carbon as inertinite-like, and therefore effectively permanent.

## Key figures

| Figure | Value | Where | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposed benchmark | Random reflectance **Ro = 2%** ("IBRo2%") | [Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409) | ✓ abstract |
| Commercial samples meeting it | **76%** of studied commercial biochars had their entire Ro distribution above 2% | [Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409) | ✓ abstract |
| Modelled persistence | ~**100 million years** for a typical inertinite biochar to lose half its carbon, assuming a highly oxidizing environment at a constant 30 °C | [Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409) | ✓ abstract |
| Other carbon pools | Incompletely carbonized biochar may hold up to three less-stable pools, measurable with geochemical pyrolysis plus Ro | [Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409) | ✓ abstract |
| Carbonization temperature | Ro can be used to back-calculate the peak temperature biochar particles reached | [Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409) | ✓ abstract |

## Use with care

- The half-life comes from a kinetic model for inertinite under stated conditions. It isn't a field measurement of biochar decay.
- Crediting reflectance is a standards decision; see [[Permanence Science for Credits]] and [[Puro vs Other Standards]]. Critiques of the approach: [[Risks and Controversies]].

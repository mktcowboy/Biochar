# PAHs

Chapter on **polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** in biochar: formation, process controls, toxicity framing, and EBC/IBI/S668 testing. Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Contaminants and Quality]] · [[Biochar Quality from Process]] · [[Pyrolysis Systems and Temperatures]] · [[Testing EBC IBI S668]] · [[Heavy Metals and Ash]] · [[Product Standards EBC IBI]] · [[Key Sources]]

## 1. Formation and process dependence

Pyrolysis of organic materials generates PAHs ([EBC 10.5E §7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). Content depends primarily on:

- Peak temperature and vapor residence
- Separation of biochar from pyrolysis gases
- **Post-pyrolysis condensation** of PAH-laden vapors onto cool porous char

EBC states feedstock type has a **negligible** influence relative to process conditions ([§7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). Clean combustion of pyrolysis gases usually destroys PAHs in the gas phase; contamination rises when gases **adsorb or condense** on the solid. Controlled vapor quenching and high-T separation help.

Even simple flame-curtain kilns can achieve low PAH with correct operation; some industrial designs have historically produced elevated PAH as a process-quality signal ([EBC §7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf); Kon-Tiki emissions/char study [Cornelissen et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0154617)).

## 2. Which PAH lists matter

| List | Role |
|---|---|
| **16 EPA PAH** | Classic monitoring suite; EU fertiliser regulation uses Σ16 = **6 mg kg⁻¹ DM** for soil amendments ([EBC citing EU 2019/1009](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)) |
| **8 EFSA PAH** | Carcinogenic subset used for tighter EBC controls |
| **REACH extras** | Benzo[e]pyrene & benzo[j]fluoranthene ≤ **1 mg kg⁻¹** each for all EBC classes |

EBC analysis of 936 certified samples found non-carcinogenic PAHs usually **>80%** of Σ16; 99% of samples meeting Σ16 ≤ 6 mg kg⁻¹ also had Σ8 EFSA < 1 mg kg⁻¹—motivating the dual limit to stop post-treatments that strip light PAHs while leaving heavy carcinogens ([EBC §7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).

## 3. EBC limit summary (v10.5E)

| Class | Σ16 EPA PAH | Σ8 EFSA PAH |
|---|---|---|
| FeedPlus, Agro, AgroOrganic | **6.0 ± 2.4 mg kg⁻¹ DM** | **1.0 mg kg⁻¹ DM** |
| Urban, Materials | Declaration of Σ16 to CSI | **1.0 mg kg⁻¹ DM** |
| Basic | Declaration | **4 mg kg⁻¹ DM** |

([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) Tab.1 / §7.11). Units in EBC tables also appear as g t⁻¹ (= mg kg⁻¹).

## 4. Bioavailability and risk posture

Hilber and co-workers (cited extensively in EBC) show soil-applied low-PAH biochar often acts as a **net sink** for environmental PAHs rather than a source; bioavailability of biochar-bound PAHs is typically very low—but rumen studies showed substantial desorption of PAHs from **high-PAH** chars, motivating strict FeedPlus limits ([EBC §7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). Absent biochar-specific risk assessments, regulators applied compost/digestate-like limits (precautionary).

**PCB / PCDD/F:** EBC requires quantification when Σ8 EFSA PAH > **1 mg kg⁻¹**; limits **0.2 mg kg⁻¹** PCB and **20 ng kg⁻¹ I-TEQ** PCDD/F ([§7.10](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). FeedPlus has stricter dioxin/PCB packages (§10.7).

## 5. Testing notes

- Standard soil PAH methods often **under-extract** biochar—use EBC-endorsed methods/labs ([EBC §7.11](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).
- North American method pointers: [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] §12.1.1 · [PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf) (EPA extraction/analysis pathways).
- IBI standards similarly require PAH declaration/limits by grade—see [IBI standards portal](https://biochar-international.org/biochar-standards/) · [[Product Standards EBC IBI]].

---

## 6. Practice takeaway

1. Engineer **no cool-down in PAH-rich vapor**.
2. Dual-track Σ16 and Σ8 (plus REACH pair) for soil/feed grades.
3. Treat elevated PAH as a **process failure**, not a feedstock inevitability.

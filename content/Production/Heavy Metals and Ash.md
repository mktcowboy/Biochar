# Heavy Metals and Ash

Chapter on **ash** and **trace metals** in biochar: concentration during pyrolysis, feedstock drivers, EBC class limits, and agronomic salinity/micronutrient issues. Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Contaminants and Quality]] · [[Woody vs Manure Ag Feedstocks]] · [[Gasification Residues]] · [[Properties]] · [[PFAS and Emerging Contaminants]] · [[Testing EBC IBI S668]] · [[Key Sources]]

## 1. Ash conservation and concentration

Biomass ash is largely **conserved** into the solid while C/H/O leave as volatiles, so metal and mineral concentrations in biochar exceed those in the parent biomass ([EBC 10.5E §7.5](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf); [Woolf 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425) eq. 2). Woolf Table 1 mean ash: wood **2.2%** vs manure **28.5%** vs sewage sludge **39.4%** ([open PDF](https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content)).

**Exception:** mercury (and some semi-volatiles) can leave in the gas phase at pyrolysis temperatures ([EBC §7.5](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).

High ash → lower FC ([[Gasification Residues]], [[Woody vs Manure Ag Feedstocks]]) and harder O/C_org analytics ([[H-Corg and O-Corg]]; [S668 §14](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)).

## 2. EBC heavy-metal limits (v10.5E Table 2)

Indicative dry-matter limits from [EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) (FeedPlus/AgroOrganic often on 88% DM basis for some elements):

| Metal | FeedPlus / AgroOrganic (stricter) | Agro / Urban / Materials | Basic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pb | 5 g t⁻¹ (88% DM) | 45 / 120 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Cd | 0.5 g t⁻¹ (88% DM) | 0.7 / 1.5 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Cu | 70 g t⁻¹ DM | 70 / 100 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Ni | 25 g t⁻¹ DM | 25 / 50 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Hg | 0.1 g t⁻¹ (88% DM) | 0.4 / 1 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Zn | 200 g t⁻¹ DM | 200 / 400 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| Cr | 70 g t⁻¹ DM | 70 / 90 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |
| As | 2 g t⁻¹ (88% DM) | 13 g t⁻¹ DM | Declaration |

Aligned with EU fertiliser regulation and national soil/chem ordinances as cited by EBC ([§7.5](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). **Cu/Zn exceedances** may be marketed as micronutrient fertilizers if labeled and rate-guided—not as ordinary Agro biochar.

Biosolids chars often fail Agro/Urban metal limits; EBC channels them toward **EBC-Basic** unless a country annex allows nutrient-to-metal ratio approaches ([§4.9](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).

## 3. Salts, EC, and agronomy

Leachate electrical conductivity estimates **salt content**—a feedstock contamination / manure signal ([EBC §7.6](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). Ash-rich chars can lime and fertilize but also salinize sensitive soils. Pair with nutrient declaration (N, P, K, Mg, Ca, Fe mandatory in EBC — §7.4).

## 4. Testing

- EBC: endorsed labs, class-specific packages.
- U.S.: EPA trace-metal methods per [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] §12.2 · [PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf).
- Always report on stated moisture basis (DM vs 88% DM for feed).

---

## 5. Practice takeaway

1. Metals track **feedstock cleanliness** more than kiln brand.
2. Concentration math: >50% mass loss roughly **doubles** non-volatile metal content—plan feedstock specs accordingly.
3. Separate **micronutrient** labeling from general soil-amendment claims when Cu/Zn are high.

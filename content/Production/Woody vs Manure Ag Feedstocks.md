# Woody vs Manure Ag Feedstocks

Chapter contrasting **woody / lignocellulosic** vs **manure and other high-ash agricultural** feedstocks for carbon fraction, nutrients, contaminants, and eligibility. Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Feedstocks]] · [[Production]] · [[Gasification Residues]] · [[Heavy Metals and Ash]] · [[Contaminants and Quality]] · [[Biochar Nutrients and Fertilizer Chemistry]] · [[Production Hub]] · [[Key Sources]]

---

## 1. Why the contrast matters

Feedstock sets baseline **ash**, **lignin**, and **nutrient** loads that pyrolysis concentrates or transforms. Woody biomass → high-C, low-ash chars optimized for CDR mass per tonne product. Manures / sludges → lower FC, higher ash and nutrients, tighter contaminant and certification constraints ([[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425); [EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §4).

---

## 2. Biomass priors (Woolf Table 1 / Phyllis2)

Mean dry-matter ash and DAF carbon for feedstock classes ([Woolf open PDF](https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content) Table 1; database [Phyllis2](https://phyllis.nl/)):

| Feedstock class | Ash (DM) mean | C (DAF) mean | Lignin mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | **2.2%** | 50.7% | 24.7% |
| Herbaceous | 7.0% | 48.9% | 11.8% |
| Wheat straw | 7.2% | 48.8% | 12.3% |
| Manure | **28.5%** | 45.2% | 11.3% |
| Sewage sludge | **39.4%** | 51.1% | 6.0% |
| Rice residues | 17.9% | 48.3% | 17.9% |
| Pits/shells/stones | 3.8% | 50.6% | 33.2% |

Ash is largely conserved into the solid, so high-ash feedstocks produce **high-ash biochars** even when organic matter carbonizes fully ([Woolf eqs. 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).

## 3. Biochar carbon fraction (Woolf Table 2)

| Feedstock | Pyrolysis FC (mean) | High-T pyro FC | Gasification FC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | **0.76** | **0.81** | 0.63 |
| Herbaceous | 0.64 | 0.66 | 0.38 |
| Manure | **0.39** | 0.39 | **0.14** |
| Sewage sludge | **0.37** | 0.38 | **0.10** |
| Rice residues | 0.47 | 0.48 | 0.20 |
| Pits/shells | 0.72 | 0.76 | 0.52 |

([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)). **CDR implication:** one tonne of manure char stores much less organic C than one tonne of wood char at similar permanence of that C.

## 4. Nutrients and agronomy

EBC notes nutrient contents can reach ~**one-third** of biochar mass; first-year plant availability often roughly **~15% of P**, **~1% of N**, **~50% of K** in cited ranges ([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §7.4). Manure chars are therefore more “fertilizer-like”; woody chars are nutrient-poor sponges that often need inoculation ([[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]]). EBC encourages keeping manure-derived nutrients in agricultural cycles rather than locking them into industrial materials ([EBC §4.8](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).

## 5. Certification / safety contrasts

| Topic | Woody / plant residues | Manure / animal by-products | Biosolids |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBC soil classes | Positive-list biomass; forest wood needs FSC/PEFC/etc. | Allowed except Feed; **H/C_org < 0.4** required | Generally **EBC-Basic** only; metals often too high for Agro/Urban |
| Heavy metals | Usually low if clean biomass | Watch Cu/Zn; concentration effect | Often limiting |
| PFAS | Low unless contaminated | Variable | Elevated risk → test ([[PFAS]]) |
| Feed grade | Possible (FeedPlus plant biomass) | Not for FeedPlus | No |

([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §4.7–4.9). Credit methodologies add their own feedstock eligibility—see [[MRV Stack]] / methodology notes, not duplicated here.

---

## 6. Practice takeaway

1. Match feedstock to goal: **CDR mass** → clean woody/low-ash; **nutrient recycling** → manures with metals/PFAS controls.
2. Always measure **FC and H/C_org** on high-ash chars rather than assuming wood defaults ([[H-Corg and O-Corg]]).
3. Track competing uses (feed, bedding, soil OM, energy) for additionality narratives ([[Feedstocks]]).

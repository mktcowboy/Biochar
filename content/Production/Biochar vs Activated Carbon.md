# Biochar vs Activated Carbon

A short comparison of two chars that look alike and do different jobs. **Activated carbon (AC)** gets an extra step, activation, that builds pores so it can adsorb. **Biochar** is made and tested to go into soil or another long-lived use and keep its carbon. Snapshot: **2026-09-21**.

## Short answer

1. **Activation is the dividing line, not the feedstock.** IUPAC defines activated carbon as a char reacted with gases (sometimes with added chemicals such as ZnCl₂) to increase its adsorptive properties ([IUPAC Gold Book](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00090); [Fitzer et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1351/pac199567030473)). IUPAC's 2024 glossary defines biochar as a pyrolysis solid made at 350–1000 °C with H/C_org < 0.7 and O/C_org < 0.4 ([[IUPAC Biochar Terminology|IUPAC biochar terminology]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2021-0106)).
2. **Activation multiplies surface area.** Biochars in a compilation of about 5,400 papers average **27 m²/g** (made below 300 °C) to **208 m²/g** (above 800 °C) ([Ippolito et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42773-020-00067-x)). In one lab study, **1.2 m²/g** raw corn stover reached **956 m²/g** after KOH activation at 800 °C ([Gale et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.1c00530)).
3. **Activation costs carbon, energy, and yield.** Life-cycle studies average about **97 MJ/kg** and **+6.6 kg CO₂e/kg** for AC versus **6.1 MJ/kg** and **−0.9 kg CO₂e/kg** for biochar ([Alhashimi & Aktas 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2016.11.016)).
4. **Neither wins everywhere.** AC and activated biochar did better than raw biochar for PFAS in the studies below; raw biochar matched AC on some metals at lower cost.
5. **Carbon-credit programs do not count AC as biochar removal.** Verra excludes biochar "processed into activated carbon," and Puro treats re-activated filters as ineligible (credit-rules table).

## What each one is

| Term | Meaning in this note | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Biochar | Pyrolysis or gasification solid kept for carbon preservation. S668 says biochar, charcoal, and biocoal can look identical and differ by **end use** | [S668 §4.1](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf) · [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] |
| Activated carbon | Char plus an activation step: steam or CO₂ ("physical"), or chemicals such as KOH, ZnCl₂, or H₃PO₄ ("chemical") | [Gold Book](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/A00090); [Wei & Li 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.0c06032) (steam); [Sørmo et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2020.144034) (steam, CO₂); [Gale et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.1c00530) (KOH); [Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c04447) (H₃PO₄) |
| Activated biochar | Biochar that has been activated. Every activated biochar is AC; not every AC is biochar, because some AC is coal-derived | [Gęca et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cis.2022.102687); [Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c04447) |
| "Activated" on a farm bag | USBI usage: charged with nutrients and microbes. No pores are built | [[USBI Ag Guidelines 2023]] · [PDF](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf) |

## Side by side

| | Biochar | Activated carbon |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | Pyrolysis or gasification at 350–1000 °C ([[IUPAC Biochar Terminology|IUPAC biochar terminology]]) | Carbonize, then activate. Studies cited here run about 500–900 °C ([Mopoung & Dejang 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-93249-x); [Wei & Li 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.0c06032); [Gale et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.1c00530)) |
| Surface area | 27–208 m²/g, dataset means by pyrolysis temperature; wood 184, manure/biosolids 52 ([Ippolito et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42773-020-00067-x), Table 1) | 427–870 m²/g for steam-activated eucalyptus at 500–700 °C in a 2,000 kg kiln ([Mopoung & Dejang 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-93249-x)); 646–1,167 m²/g for KOH-activated corn stover ([Gale et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.1c00530), Table 1) |
| Yield | Set by pyrolysis ([[Pyrolysis Systems and Temperatures]]) | Falls again in activation. Pinewood char steam-activated for 30 min kept 13% of the char at 700 °C, 6% at 800 °C, 3% at 900 °C (lab scale) ([Wei & Li 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.0c06032), Table 7) |
| Energy and CO₂e | 6.1 MJ/kg; −0.9 kg CO₂e/kg | 97 MJ/kg; +6.6 kg CO₂e/kg ([Alhashimi & Aktas 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2016.11.016)) |
| Price signal | About $246/ton non-activated ([Inyang & Dickenson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemosphere.2015.03.072)); $1.06/kg modeled production cost ([Shaheen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e12388)) | About $1,500/ton ([Inyang & Dickenson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemosphere.2015.03.072)); $1.34/kg modeled production cost ([Shaheen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e12388)) |
| Main use | Soil amendment and long-lived carbon storage ([S668 §4.1](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)) | Adsorption, for example drinking-water PFAS, VOCs, taste and odor ([EPA 2024](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-04/pfas-npdwr_fact-sheet_treatment_4.8.24.pdf)) |

Prices are study-specific, not spot quotes. For a vault example of delivered biochar, see $240/ton wet in [[Agronomic Economics]].

## When to use which

| Goal | Usually better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Soil carbon storage or credits | Biochar | AC is built to be used up, and credit rules exclude it (next section) |
| Soil fertility, water holding, compost blends | Biochar | See [[Soil and Agriculture]] and [[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]] |
| PFAS in drinking water | Granular AC | EPA lists GAC as a best available technology; powdered AC was left off because PFAS removal is variable ([EPA 2024](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-04/pfas-npdwr_fact-sheet_treatment_4.8.24.pdf)) |
| PFAS in contaminated soil | Activated biochar or AC | Leaching cuts scaled with degree of activation, and activation cost yield ([Sørmo et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2020.144034)). After aging tests, AC-based sorbents held >85% PFAS immobilization in most scenarios while biochar was most affected ([Kabiri et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhazmat.2026.142258)). See [[PFAS]] |
| Heavy metals in water | Case by case | Biochar was cheaper per unit removed for Cr and Zn, comparable for Pb and Cu ([Alhashimi & Aktas 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2016.11.016)); activation raised metal adsorption capacity by 136% on average across 321 paired observations ([Pathy et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2022.161252)) |
| Herbicide protection | AC on purpose; biochar by accident | AC seed pellets raised native-grass biomass by more than 4,000% under herbicide in a grow-room test ([Svejcar et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants13213074)). Biochar at 1% or 10% w/w raised herbicide sorption and cut efficacy ([Clay et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/03601234.2016.1170540)); fresh biochar at 0.05% w/w has been reported to do the same for pesticides ([Kookana 2010](https://doi.org/10.1071/sr10007)) |

## Carbon-credit rules

| Program | What it says about activation |
|---|---|
| EBC 10.5E | Thermal activation (making AC) counts as processing after production and needs EBC certification as a processing company ([EBC §13.1–13.2](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)) |
| Verra VM0044 v1.2 | Not applicable when biochar is "processed into activated carbon" or otherwise substantially oxidized (condition 14). Non-soil uses that lose more than 50% of the carbon are ineligible, with some AC as the example (condition 15 and the non-soil definition) ([VM0044 v1.2 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/VM44_v1.2_clean-1.pdf), pp. 8 and 14) · [[Verra VM0044 Methodology and Eligibility]] |
| Puro Edition 2025 (v2) | Activation must be described in the monitoring plan (rule 3.5.30). A filter that is re-activated, then landfilled or incinerated, is an ineligible cascade because re-activation "likely re-emits biochar carbon." Filter media earns credits only when its end of life preserves the carbon (Table 3.2) ([Puro PDF](https://7518557.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7518557/Puro%20Biochar%20Methodology%20-%20Edition%202025%20%28version%202%29%20-%20For%20Publication.pdf), PDF pp. 49, 59, 71) · [[Puro Biochar Methodology Edition 2025]] |

## Words to be careful with

- **"AC is just biochar" or "biochar is just AC."** Only activated biochar is both. Coal-derived AC is not biochar ([Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c04447) compares anthracite-derived and biomass-derived AC).
- **"Activated biochar" on a soil bag.** In farm usage this usually means nutrient-charged, not pore-built ([USBI Ag Guidelines](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf)). Ask which. See [[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]].
- **"More surface area is better."** EBC says BET is often over- and misinterpreted and that higher BET does not necessarily mean more contaminant binding ([EBC §7.9](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). S668 adds that no single test gives true surface area and N₂ at 77 K misses pores below 0.45 nm ([S668 §11.3](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)).
- **"AC stores carbon."** The credit rules above say otherwise, and exhausted GAC can be landfilled, incinerated, or reactivated ([EPA 2024](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-04/pfas-npdwr_fact-sheet_treatment_4.8.24.pdf)).

## Limits of this note

- The IUPAC biochar glossary PDF was unreachable on 2026-09-21, so no glossary entry on activated biochar is quoted here.
- Cost and energy figures are study-specific. Shaheen et al. built the activated-carbon side of their comparison from literature process data ([Shaheen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e12388)).
- Text searches of the IPCC 2019 appendix, S668, NRCS CPS 336 (standard and FAQ), and the Isometric Biochar Protocol v1.3 page found no activation language, so this vault has no permanence factor for activated biochar ([[Permanence Factors Fperm]]).

# PFAS and Emerging Contaminants

Chapter on **per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS)** and related emerging organics in biochar systems—especially biosolids and waste-derived feedstocks. Distinguishes **solid-phase removal** from **true destruction**. Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Contaminants and Quality]] · [[Heavy Metals and Ash]] · [[Woody vs Manure Ag Feedstocks]] · [[Testing EBC IBI S668]] · [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] · [[Risks and Controversies]] · [[Key Sources]]

---

## 1. Why PFAS shows up in biochar conversations

PFAS are ubiquitous and especially elevated in **wastewater treatment plant biosolids**. Pyrolysis is explored both as a biosolids management path and as a potential PFAS treatment. [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] §12.1.4 states PFAS are more common in biochar from high-PFAS feedstocks such as WWTP biosolids and recommends **periodic analysis** when those feeds are used ([PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)).

EBC allows biosolids for **EBC-Basic** with worker-safety plans and minimum **>500 °C for 3 min**, noting metals usually block Agro/Urban soil use ([EBC 10.5E §4.9](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)). PFAS is an additional emerging constraint beyond metals.

## 2. Removal ≠ destruction (critical distinction)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Pyrolysis can remove **>99%** of targeted PFAS from the **solid** (biochar) phase | McNamara et al. 2023 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D2EW00677D) |
| Some PFAS / precursors can **increase in py-liquid** (e.g. NEtFOSE, NMeFOSE masses rose >100× vs influent biosolids across 500–800 °C trials) | Same study |
| Pyrolysis + **thermal oxidizer** on off-gas achieved **99.4%** molar PFAS removal in a lab system resembling full-scale; short-chain PFAS dominated residual flue gas | Thomsen et al. / *Water Environ. Res.* — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/wer.11149) |
| Bench pyrolysis/co-pyrolysis at 600 °C reported **99.4%** PFAS destruction efficiency under long residence (~10 s gas / 1 h solids) with residual char PFAS meeting proposed unrestricted land-app limits in that study | Rathnayake et al. 2025 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaap.2025.106970) |
| Reviews: destruction efficacy depends on PFAS structure, T, O₂, residence; incomplete combustion products are a concern | Longendyke et al. 2022 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D1EM00465D) |

**Vault takeaway:** low PFAS in biochar is necessary but not sufficient for a clean process claim—track **liquids and stack gas**, and use thermal oxidation where required.

## 3. Testing guidance (methods, not pass/fail)

[[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] ([PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf) §12.1.4):

- Analyze per **EPA Method 1633A** when testing biochar for PFAS.
- Standard **does not** set pass/fail criteria—use jurisdictional guidance (e.g. EPA interim biosolids limits, state soil screening levels).
- Matrix challenges: high organic C and inorganics affect extraction; contamination control is essential; science is still emerging.

Pair with metals and PAH packages for biosolids-derived chars ([[Heavy Metals and Ash]], [[PAHs]]).

## 4. Other emerging / regulated organics (brief)

- **Dioxins/furans & PCBs:** covered under PAH-triggered EBC rules and FeedPlus packages ([[PAHs]]; [EBC §7.10, §10.7](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)); S668 lists EPA 8082A / 8290A pathways ([§12.1.2–12.1.3](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)).
- **Pharmaceuticals / micropollutants in manures:** EBC uses high carbonization (H/C_org < 0.4) as a destruction proxy for animal by-products ([§4.8, §10.4](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).
- **Microplastics in feedstock:** EBC mass limits and possible C-sink deductions ([§4.5](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf)).

---

## 5. Practice takeaway

1. Any **biosolids → biochar** pathway needs a PFAS mass-balance story (solid + liquid + gas), not only a clean char certificate.
2. Prefer feeds with low PFAS when targeting soil grades; reserve contaminated feeds for tightly engineered systems with thermal oxidizers.
3. Cite method (EPA 1633A) and jurisdiction when reporting PFAS results.

# Feedstocks

Brief hub for biomass inputs to biochar. Existing notes already cover how feedstock changes carbon fraction and chemistry; this page tracks what still needs dedicated research rather than inventing inventory data.

## What we already know (in vault)

- [[Production]] — feedstock is a primary control alongside temperature and residence time; woody/low-ash chars often ~50–90% C; manure/sludge and gasification residues are much lower ([[Woolf et al. 2021|Woolf et al., 2021]] examples in that note).
- [[Properties]] — ash, nutrients, pH, and CEC vary widely with feedstock class; H/C_org and O/C_org remain the usual persistence proxies.
- [[Contaminants and Quality]] — sludge, treated wood, and some manures raise contaminant and eligibility questions for product standards and credit methodologies.

## Why feedstock choice matters

| Dimension | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Carbon yield & permanence proxies | Sets how much durable C per tonne feedstock |
| Contaminants | PAH / heavy-metal risk; eligibility under [EBC](https://www.european-biochar.org/)/[IBI](https://biochar-international.org/) and registries |
| Competing uses | Biomass may already be fuel, feed, or soil organic matter |
| Logistics | Moisture, density, seasonality, and transport cost |
| Additionality / baseline | Credit programs care whether feedstock would have decayed or burned |

## Gaps / to research later

- Systematic taxonomy: wood residues, ag residues, manures, biosolids, dedicated crops, invasive biomass
- Typical moisture/ash ranges and preprocessing (drying, chipping, pelletizing)
- How major methodologies restrict or score feedstocks (see [[Puro Biochar Methodology Edition 2025]], [[Verra VM0044]], [[Isometric Biochar Protocol]], [[CSI Global Biochar C-Sink]])
- US regional supply (link [[NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag]] for farm-side context)
- Sustainability screens: ILUC, biodiversity, food competition — research only; no invented criteria here

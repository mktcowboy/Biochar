## 1. Process definition

Thermochemical decomposition of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin under oxygen-free or oxygen-limited conditions yields:

1. **Solid** — biochar (pyrogenic carbon-rich porous solid)
2. **Liquids** — bio-oil / pyroligneous fractions
3. **Gases** — CO₂, CO, H₂, light hydrocarbons (syngas when oxidant is present at gasification levels)

**IPCC inventory definition:** solid from heating biomass **>350 °C** under controlled/limited oxidant so it does not combust (pyrolysis **or** gasification), for mineral soils in cropland/grassland ([[IPCC Biochar Soil Carbon Method|estimates soil C stock change from biochar]] · [PDF](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2019rf/pdf/4_Volume4/19R_V4_Ch02_Ap4_Biochar.pdf)).

**IUPAC (Bilias et al. 2024):** solid multifunctional porous carbonaceous product of agricultural biomass pyrolysis typically **350–1000 °C**, with molar **H/C_org < 0.7** and **O/C_org < 0.4** ([[IUPAC Biochar Terminology|IUPAC biochar terminology]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2021-0106)).

**Not biochar under these definitions:** torrefaction / HTC solids (typically <350 °C or wet pressurized; not sufficiently persistent for Woolf/IPCC methods — [Woolf 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425) §2.1.1), fossil chars, open wildfire charcoal without process control.

EBC treats **gasification** as within the pyrolysis technology spectrum when optimized for biochar and otherwise compliant ([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §2).

## 2. Typical modes

| Mode | Temperature / rate | Favors | Deep note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slow pyrolysis** | ~300–700 °C, low heating rate, minutes–hours | Biochar yield | [[Pyrolysis Systems and Temperatures]] |
| **Fast / flash pyrolysis** | Higher heating rates, seconds | Liquids (bio-oil) | Same |
| **Gasification** | Higher T, limited oxidant | Syngas; solid often high-ash / lower FC | [[Gasification Residues]] |

Controls on properties: feedstock class, peak temperature, residence time, heating rate, reactor design, ash ([[Biochar Quality from Process]]; [Keiluweit et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1021/es9031419)).

## 3. Carbon content (FC) — Woolf examples

Organic carbon mass fraction on dry matter varies from as low as **~7%** (gasified biosolids) to **~79%** (wood pyrolysed >600 °C) in Woolf’s abstract range ([[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)). Table 2 class means ([open PDF](https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content)):

| Feedstock / process | Approx. FC |
|---|---|
| Wood pyrolysis (mean of T classes) | **0.76** |
| Wood high-T (≥600 °C) | **0.81** |
| Wood low / medium | **0.70 / 0.77** |
| Manure pyrolysis (all T ≈) | **0.39** |
| Sewage sludge pyrolysis mean | **0.37** |
| Gasification wood | **0.63** |
| Gasification manure / sludge | **~0.14 / 0.10** |

Full feedstock grid: [[Woody vs Manure Ag Feedstocks]] · [[Feedstocks]]. Biomass ash/lignin priors: Woolf Table 1 from Phyllis2 ([https://phyllis.nl/](https://phyllis.nl/)).

## 4. Process effects on quality and permanence (summary)

Higher pyrolysis T → lower H/C_org & O/C_org, higher aromatic condensation, generally higher \(F_\mathrm{perm}\), often higher pH/surface area (then possible decline at very high T), lower volatile matter, sometimes lower mass yield ([Keiluweit 2010](https://doi.org/10.1021/es9031419); [Woolf 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425); [[Permanence]]). Gasification residues can be very high-ash / low FC; permanence of the **organic C** may still be high if H/C_org is low, but C per tonne product is lower ([[Gasification Residues]]).

PAH risk is driven more by **vapor management** than feedstock identity ([[PAHs]]; [EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §7.11).

## 5. EBC process floor (sustainability, not just chemistry)

For EBC-certified production ([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §8): recover/use excess heat or liquid/gaseous products; **do not** release pyrolysis gases without a dedicated combustion chamber; meet national emission limits; document homogeneous batch quality (temperature typically held within **±20%** of declared set-point — §5); energy-efficiency target ≥**60%** of feedstock+expenditures recovered in useful products (with transitional exemptions).

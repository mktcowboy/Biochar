# Crop Yield Effects

Yield responses to biochar are **context-specific**—stronger in acid, coarse-textured, degraded, and **tropical** soils; weaker or null on average in many **temperate fertile** systems. Biochar is **not** a universal fertilizer substitute. Pair with [[Soil and Agriculture]], [[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]], [[Application Rates and Methods]], [[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]], and [[Agronomic Economics]].

### Mechanistic subgroups (Jeffery 2011)

From the 2011 meta ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015)):

- **Acidic soils ~+14%**, neutral ~+13%; liming hypothesized as a major pathway.
- **Coarse ~+10%**, medium ~+13% textures; water holding implicated.
- **100 t ha⁻¹** subgroup showed the largest positive mean (**+39%**) — research extreme, not a farm recommendation.
- **Poultry-litter** feedstock subgroup strong positive (~**+28%**); **biosolids** feedstock the only significant **negative** feedstock mean (~**−28%**).

### Tropical vs temperate (Jeffery 2017)

Jeffery et al. (2017) conclude biochar **boosts tropical but not temperate** mean crop yields, attributing tropical gains largely to liming and nutrient effects in acidic, nutrient-poor soils ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd); open PDF mirror: https://ecoss.nau.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Jeffery_2017_Environ._Res._Lett._12_053001.pdf).

## Field trials and real deployments (selected)

Details and more URLs: [[Real World Soil Projects]].

### Oasis Vineyard (Monterey County, CA) — multi-year

Pre-plant treatments on Pinot Noir (Oceano Sand / Garey Sandy Loam, ~0.7% OM): control; compost **15 t/ac**; biochar **10 t/ac**; compost+biochar. Same irrigation/fertilizer regime. Across **five harvests**, average yield increases vs control: compost+biochar **+36%**, biochar **+25%**, compost **+29%** (combined five-season totals 41.7 / 38.2 / 40.1 vs 31.7 t/ac). Quality not significantly degraded. Primary report: https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Oasis-Vineyard-Trial_-5th-harvest_-Final-Draft_12.12.2023.pdf · [[Oasis Vineyard Trial 2023]]

### Exomad Green field study (Santa Cruz, Bolivia) — 2025 reporting

Company report with CIAT at three Santa Cruz sites: one **4 t ha⁻¹** biochar application, beans then corn, under drought. Reported gains vs untreated control: corn **+15%** biochar alone, up to **+32%** with NPKS + microorganisms; beans **+13%** alone, up to **+25%** combined ([PDF](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf); [Bioenergy International 2025-07-04](https://bioenergyinternational.com/biochar-field-study-reveals-significant-crop-yield-improvements-in-bolivia/)). **Caveats:** the report's own yield ANOVA is not significant (beans p = 0.4481, corn p = 0.924); there is no fertilizer-only arm; and recomputed against the control, bean gains are **+14.9%** alone and **+34.2%** combined. Aligns with tropical/acid-soil expectation from Jeffery 2017. Full breakdown: [[Exomad Green Corn and Bean Field Study]]. Facility/soil end-use: [Puro Exomad Concepción](https://puro.earth/cdr-credit-suppliers/facilities/exomad-green-concepcion/).

### Nepal — enriched biochar fertilizers (low dose)

- [[Urine-Enhanced Biochar Field Trial|urine-enhanced biochar field trial]]: urine-enhanced biochar + compost produced very large pumpkin yield responses relative to urine or biochar alone on a fertile tropical soil ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture5030723)).
- Pandit / Schmidt et al.: **21 field trials**, **13 crop species**, liquid nutrient–enriched biochar fertilizers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761) · [[Liquid Nutrient Enrichment Field Trials|liquid nutrient enrichment trials]]).

These support the [[Soil and Agriculture]] point that **charged / biochar-based fertilizers** can work at **≪ bulk research rates**.

### Brazil — degraded pastures

Latawiec et al. (2019) evaluate biochar on degraded pasturelands with environmental and cost-benefit analysis ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-47647-x); PDF: https://www.iis-rio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Latawiec_et_al-2019-Scientific_Reports.pdf).

## Inoculated / enriched vs raw (pointer)

Yield metas that mix raw and fertilized biochar understate the **charging** effect. Dedicated evidence:

| Contrast | Result | URL |
|---|---|---|

## When yields fail to rise

- Temperate, already-fertile, near-neutral pH soils ([Jeffery 2017](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd)).
- Fresh, high-sorption char without compost/manure charge ([NRCS FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Contaminated or inappropriate feedstocks (biosolids subgroup negative in Jeffery 2011; PFAS → biosolids **excluded** under CPS 336).
- Alkaline soils with high-liming chars (FAQ).
- Economics: even positive yield deltas may not repay purchase price without carbon finance — [[Agronomic Economics]], [Galinato et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2011.07.035).

# Nitrous Oxide Fluxes

Agricultural framing of how soil-applied biochar changes **nitrous oxide (N₂O)** emissions. Hub: [[GHG Fluxes]]. Sisters: [[Methane Fluxes]], [[Priming and SOC Interactions]]. CDR tonnage is still dominated by **stable biochar C** ([[Permanence]], [[Biochar as Durable CDR]]).

Report context: **2026-09-16**.

## 1. Summary numbers

| Source | Mean / rule | Scope | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cayuela et al. 2014 | **−54%** | 30 studies, 261 treatments; lab + field mixed (2007–2013 window) | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2013.10.009 · [[Nitrous Oxide Mitigation Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes N2O mitigation]] · author PDF https://digital.csic.es/bitstream/10261/273052/4/Cayuela2014AgricEcosysEnv.pdf |
| Jia et al. 2023 | **−26.8%** | Global meta; 3,883 paired obs / 220 studies; pools incubation, pot, **and** field | https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.4868 · [[Global GHG and Yield Patterns|global GHG and yield patterns]] |
| Woolf et al. 2021 inventory | Optional **−23%** (95% CI 5–41%), **year 1 only**, only if **>10 Mg C ha⁻¹**; field-fitted | https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425 · [[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]] |
| Woolf weighing of prior metas | Cites Cayuela −54%; Verhoeven −12.4%; Borchard −38%; Verhoeven reanalysis −9.2% ns | same §3.3.2 | same |

**Farm takeaway:** treat −54% as an early lab-heavy ceiling, −26.8% as a broader modern mean (still mixing study types), and Woolf’s −23% as a **conservative inventory option** that most CPS 336 planning rates **do not unlock**.

## 2. Mechanisms

### 2.1 Electron shuttling / denitrification

Cayuela et al. (2013) *Scientific Reports* examine biochar’s role in soil denitrification pathways ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep01732)).

### 2.2 H:C_org as a product moderator

Cayuela et al. (2015): **molar H:C_org** is a key factor—more aromatic / lower H:C_org chars tend to mitigate N₂O more strongly ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2014.12.015)). This is one scientific reason NRCS and EBC/IBI stress **H:C_org < 0.7** ([[Soil Carbon Amendment and US Ag|NRCS soil carbon amendment in US ag]], [[Product Standards EBC IBI]], [[Properties]]).

### 2.3 Rate, texture, N form

Cayuela 2014: application rate directly correlated with N₂O reductions; soil texture and chemical form of N fertilizer strongly influence emissions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2013.10.009)). Jia 2023: biochar-induced soil **pH** change was the strongest driver of GHG responses in that analysis ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.4868)).

### 2.4 Fertilizer N baselines

[[Fertilizer-Controlled Yield Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes yields with fertilizer controls]] emphasize fertilizer interactions for yield ([PDF](https://www.css.cornell.edu/faculty/lehmann/publ/SoilUseManage%2036%2C%202-18%202020%20Ye.pdf)); N rate also modulates N₂O baselines. Nutrient chemistry: [[Biochar Nutrients and Fertilizer Chemistry]].

## 3. Inventory / MRV conservatism (Woolf 2021)

Woolf et al. (2021) greenhouse-gas inventory method for biochar soil additions:

- Optional N₂O adjustment **−23%** for the **first year only**
- Only when application exceeds **10 Mg C ha⁻¹**
- Fitted excluding pot/incubation studies
- Assume **no net CH₄ change** for inventory simplicity
- **Mineral soils only** — organic/forest soils with organic horizons **out of scope** (positive priming possible)
- Priming of native SOC **not credited** (net negative priming not statistically robust in metas they cite)

([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425); open PDF https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content · [[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]])

**Rate reality check:** CPS 336 planning often starts at **4–10 yd³/ac** ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). With dry bulk density ~135–540 lb/yd³, many farm rates are **below** 10 Mg C ha⁻¹—so the optional inventory N₂O term often **does not apply**. Credit programs primarily remunerate **durable C**, not flux co-benefits ([[MRV Stack]], [[Matrix Pathways for Credits]]).

## 4. Interactions with blends and management

- Compost / manure blends change labile C, moisture, and N availability that drive N₂O — [[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]].
- Facility composting N₂O cuts (e.g. Li et al. 2025 mean composting N₂O **−49.8%** — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48130/nc-0025-0003)) are **not** soil flux results.
- Earth Foundries **VOC −33%** is a composting-process metric ([PDF](https://www.earthfoundriesinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Earth-Foundries-Green-Compost-Biochar-Composting-Project-Report-2023.pdf)).
- Co-composted particles that capture nitrate did not stimulate N₂O in Hagemann PLOS experiments ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171214))—context-specific.

## 6. Comparing the three “headline” numbers

| Number | What it averages | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **−54%** (Cayuela 2014) | Early literature; lab + field; 261 treatments | Historical benchmark; upper-end illustration |
| **−26.8%** (Jia 2023) | Broader modern corpus; still mixes incubation/pot/field | Prefer for “recent global mean” language |
| **−23% year 1** (Woolf 2021) | Field-fitted inventory option at **>10 Mg C ha⁻¹** only | National inventory / conservative MRV — usually **not** CPS 336 rates |

## 7. What not to do in farm NPV

1. Do not multiply Cayuela −54% by fertilizer N₂O emission factors for 10-year cash-flow models without site data.
2. Do not apply Woolf’s optional term below **10 Mg C ha⁻¹** or beyond year 1 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).
3. Do not treat composting N₂O metas as soil results ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.48130/nc-0025-0003)).
4. Do not ignore H:C_org — low-aromatic chars may underperform Cayuela 2015 expectations ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2014.12.015)).
5. Prefer on-farm chambers if flux co-benefits are in a contract ([[On-farm Trial Design]]).

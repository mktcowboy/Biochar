# Exomad Green Corn and Bean Field Study

Study note on **"Biochar Field Study — Corn and Beans in Controlled Plots"**, a 13-page field report published by Exomad Green and run with **CIAT** (Centro de Investigación Agrícola Tropical) at three sites in Santa Cruz, Bolivia. Biochar was applied once at **4 t ha⁻¹** before a winter bean crop (2024); corn followed with no re-application ([PDF](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf)). Exomad accounts for roughly **60%** of biochar CDR tonnes sold since 2022 ([CDR.fyi Snapshot 2025](https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/biochar-carbon-removal-market-snapshot-2025)); this is one of the field-study PDFs on its [R&D page](https://www.exomadgreen.com/research-development). Report context: **2026-09-14**.

## Bottom line

- **Headline gains (as reported):** biochar alone **+15%** corn and **+13%** beans; biochar + NPKS + microorganisms **+32%** corn and **+25%** beans vs an untreated control ([PDF p.8](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=8); [p.10](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=10)). Direction fits meta-analyses of low-fertility tropical soils ([[Tropical vs Temperate Yields|compares tropical vs temperate yields]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd)).
- **Yield differences are not statistically significant.** The report's own ANOVA gives yield **p = 0.4481** (beans) and **p = 0.924** (corn). Only bean plant height (p < 0.0001) and corn 100-grain weight (p = 0.014) differ significantly (same pages).
- **Only T2 vs T1 isolates biochar.** There is no fertilizer-only arm, so the larger T3/T4 gains cannot be credited to biochar.
- **Bean percentages are understated.** Against the control, beans gain **+14.9% / +24.9% / +34.2%** (T2/T3/T4), not the printed 12.94 / 19.90 / 25.47% — the report divided by treatment yield instead of control yield (§6.1).
- **Soil-health conclusions come without soil data.** Better nutrient availability, porosity, moisture retention, leaching, CEC, and pH are asserted ([PDF p.13](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=13)), but no post-trial soil tests are shown.
- **Use it as** supportive company case evidence with these caveats attached — not as proof of a yield response.

## 2. Treatments

| Code | Treatment (as printed) | Adds vs previous arm |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Control (no intervention) | — |
| T2 | Single application of biochar **without** microorganisms | Raw biochar |
| T3 | Biochar + NPKS fertilizers | + fertilizer |
| T4 | Biochar + NPKS + microorganisms | + microbial inoculant |

Source: [PDF p.5](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=5). Exomad says its biochar is "usually activated" with microbes including *Rhizobium*, *Pseudomonas*, *Azospirillum*, and mycorrhizae ([PDF p.2](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=2)); the T4 inoculant's product, dose, and timing are not given.

**Missing arms:** fertilizer only, microbes only, and fertilizer + microbes without biochar. For contrast, [[Fertilizer-Controlled Yield Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes yields with fertilizer controls]] measure the ~**+15%** biochar effect against **fertilizer alone** ([PDF](https://www.css.cornell.edu/faculty/lehmann/publ/SoilUseManage%2036%2C%202-18%202020%20Ye.pdf)) — the comparison this design cannot make.

## 3. Biochar used (as reported)

| Parameter | Value | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Organic carbon | **86.3%** | NRCS CPS 336 requires C_org > 10% ([CPS 336 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf) · [[NRCS CPS 336 Standard 2022]]) |
| H:C_org | **0.24** | Under the 0.7 limit in the IUPAC definition and NRCS ([[IUPAC Biochar Terminology\|IUPAC biochar terminology]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/pac-2021-0106)); under Isometric soil < 0.5 ([module](https://registry.isometric.com/module/biochar-storage-soil-environments/1.3)) and CSI ≤ 0.40 for GPC ([CSI 3.3](https://www.carbon-standards.com/docs/transfer/4000039EN.pdf)) |
| Ash | **8.6%** | — |
| pH | **7.2** | Near-neutral; NRCS notes biochar is usually alkaline and valued for liming acid soils — liming equivalence not reported ([NRCS FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf) · [[NRCS CPS 336 FAQ 2026]]) |
| Moisture | **18%** | Dose basis matters (below) |

Values: [PDF p.2](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=2). **Not reported:** feedstock, pyrolysis temperature, particle size, lab or method, sampling date, or whether H:C_org is molar. Exomad's Concepción facility listing describes sawmill hardwood residues pyrolyzed at ~600 °C ([Puro facility page](https://puro.earth/cdr-credit-suppliers/facilities/exomad-green-concepcion/)), but the report doesn't say which facility supplied the trial char. Persistence context: biochars with H/C_org < 0.5 retained a mean ~82% of C after 100 years in [[Biochar in Climate Change Mitigation|reviews biochar climate mitigation]] ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00852-8)) — see [[Permanence]] and [[Properties]].

**Applied carbon (vault arithmetic, not a credit figure):** 4 t ha⁻¹ × 86.3% C ≈ **3.45 t C ≈ 12.7 t CO₂ ha⁻¹** gross if the dose is dry-basis; ≈ **2.83 t C ≈ 10.4 t CO₂ ha⁻¹** if it is as-received at 18% moisture. Credited tonnes apply persistence factors and life-cycle deductions — [[Puro Biochar Methodology Edition 2025]], [[Permanence Science for Credits]].

Source: [PDF p.4](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=4) (CIAT Water, Soil and Plant Laboratory). Sampling depth and date are not given. **Reading:** CRI and EEAS are mildly acidic, low-OM, low-CEC soils — the tropical low-fertility setting where meta-analyses report larger yield responses ([Jeffery 2017](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd); [[Crop Productivity Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes crop productivity]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015)). CL is a high-clay, higher-P soil. Results are not broken out by site, so these contrasts can't be tested from the report.

### 6.2 Corn (harvest; drought season, no new biochar)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Control | 156 | 24.5 | 1,962.27 | — | — |
| T2 Biochar | 165 | 27.5 | 2,249.90 | +287.63 | **+14.7%** |
| T3 + NPKS | 169 | 29.0 | 2,259.11 | +296.84 | **+15.1%** |
| T4 + NPKS + microbes | **128** | 30.5 | 2,590.67 | +628.40 | **+32.0%** |
| CV % | 7.95 | 7.9 | **57.49** | | |
| ANOVA p | "<0.44" (as printed) | **0.014** | **0.924** | | |

Source: [PDF p.10](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=10); ear photo and drought statement on [p.11](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=11). T4 plants were **~18% shorter** than the control yet highest-yielding; the report doesn't explain why. A 57.49% yield CV implies a residual SD of ~1,300 kg ha⁻¹ on a ~2,270 kg ha⁻¹ mean (vault arithmetic) — consistent with site or drought variation left in the error term, but the model isn't described.

### 6.3 What each contrast isolates (vault arithmetic from table means)

| Contrast | Isolates | Beans | Corn |
|---|---|---|---|
| T2 − T1 | Raw biochar | +176.8 kg ha⁻¹ (+14.9%) | +287.6 (+14.7%) — second-season residual |
| T3 − T2 | NPKS fertilizer, given biochar | +118.7 (+8.7%) | +9.2 (+0.4%) |
| T4 − T3 | Microbial inoculant, given biochar + NPKS | +110.8 (+7.5%) | +331.6 (+14.7%) |
| T3 − T1 | Biochar + NPKS **combined** (no fertilizer-only arm to split them) | +295.6 (+24.9%) | +296.8 (+15.1%) |
| T4 − T1 | Whole package | +406.4 (+34.2%) | +628.4 (+32.0%) |

No yield difference is backed by a significant ANOVA in either crop (§6.1–6.2). In corn, 100 kg N ha⁻¹ added almost nothing on top of biochar (T3 vs T2 +0.4%). That would fit water rather than nutrients limiting a drought season, but the report gives no rainfall or soil-moisture data to check.

## 8. Data-quality issues

1. **Non-significant yields** (beans p = 0.4481; corn p = 0.924), with no mean-separation tests; replication, plot size, and statistical model undescribed ([PDF p.8](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=8); [p.10](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=10)).
2. **Confounded treatments** — no fertilizer-only or microbes-only arms (§2).
3. **Mixed percentage bases** — beans ÷ treatment, corn ÷ control (§6.1). Press coverage repeated the understated bean figures ([Bioenergy International](https://bioenergyinternational.com/biochar-field-study-reveals-significant-crop-yield-improvements-in-bolivia/); [Carbon Herald](https://carbonherald.com/in-a-new-field-study-exomad-green-finds-biochar-improved-corn-and-bean-crops/)).
4. **Date conflict** — corn is printed as planted Nov 2023–Jan 2024, before the June 2024 bean crop it followed. June planting + 110–120 days puts bean harvest around late Sep–mid Oct 2024, so Nov 2024–Jan 2025 is the likely intent — unconfirmed ([PDF p.6](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=6)).
5. **Charts disagree with tables** — e.g., corn 100-grain-weight bars read ≈19 / 28 / 37 / 37 g against 24.5 / 27.5 / 29.0 / 30.5 g in the table ([PDF p.12](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=12) vs [p.10](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=10)); bean flower bars also drift ([p.9](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=9)). Use table values.
6. **Soil-health claims without data** — no post-trial pH, CEC, OM, bulk density, or moisture measurements ([PDF p.13](https://www.exomadgreen.com/_files/ugd/8b0a69_153ad8a2b8244bfc8a136176be56a408.pdf#page=13)).
7. **Not reported** — per-site results, bean species or variety, corn hybrid, incorporation depth, particle size, inoculant product and dose, weather, yield moisture basis.
8. **Conflict of interest** — a producer and credit seller reporting its own trial; CIAT is named as collaborator, but no CIAT authors, sign-off, or raw data appear in the PDF.

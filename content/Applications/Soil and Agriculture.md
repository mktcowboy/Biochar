# Soil and Agriculture

Chapter-length map of **biochar in farm and soil systems**: how it changes soil, what yield and GHG metas say, how to apply it, named real-world deployments, products/practices, constraints, and links to [[Permanence]], [[Soil Carbon Amendment and US Ag|NRCS soil carbon amendment in US ag]], and carbon-market soil pathways.

## Purpose and scope

Most historical agronomy and a large share of voluntary biochar CDR still assume **soil end-use**. Outcomes are **context-specific** (texture, pH, climate, rate, feedstock/process, fertilizer regime). This note synthesizes primary metas, NRCS practice guidance, and named projects—not universal prescriptions.

## 1. Soil mechanisms

### 1.1 Water retention and physical structure

Meta-analyses generally show **larger hydrological benefits in coarse-textured soils**:

- Omondi et al. (2016) quantified mean effects including **available water capacity (AWC) +15.1%**, **porosity +8.4%**, **bulk density −7.6%**, and **saturated hydraulic conductivity +25.2%** across compiled studies ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2016.03.029)).
- Razzaghi et al. (2020) systematic review/meta-analysis asks whether biochar improves water retention and finds texture- and rate-dependent gains ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2019.114055)).
- Wei et al. (2023) (939 observations) report **field capacity / AWC** responses by texture: coarse **FC +23.8% / AWC +25.6%**; medium **+5.0% / +20.9%**; fine **+7.2% / +11.5%** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2023.116591)).
- Blanco-Canqui (2017) reviews physical-property pathways (bulk density, porosity, aggregation, hydraulics) as functions of rate, feedstock, and soil ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.2136/sssaj2017.01.0017)).

**Practice implication:** sandy / low-OM soils are the clearest hydrological use-case; fine clays may show smaller AWC gains for the same rate.

### 1.2 Nutrients, CEC, and liming / pH

[[Crop Productivity Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes crop productivity]] interpreted stronger productivity responses in **acidic** (+14%) and **coarse/medium** soils as consistent with **liming** and **water-holding** mechanisms, plus improved nutrient availability ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015)). [[Tropical vs Temperate Yields|compares tropical vs temperate yields]] found mean tropical yield benefits (~**+25%**) versus little mean benefit in temperate systems, again consistent with acid / low-fertility soil chemistry ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd)).

USDA NRCS CPS 336 guidance (April 2026 FAQ) states biochar is **usually alkaline** and most useful as a liming agent on acid soils; on soils already **pH > 7**, check **liming equivalency** and add cautiously. The FAQ walks liming-offset arithmetic (e.g., 1 ton biochar/acre at 9% CaCO₃ liming equivalency offsets ~9% of a 1 ton lime recommendation) ([NRCS FAQ PDF](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf) · [[Soil Carbon Amendment FAQ|answers CPS 336 planning questions]]).

NRCS also flags **electrical conductivity (EC) / salts**: high-ash manure chars can raise salinity; wood chars are typically lower EC. Spec-sheet examples in the FAQ contrast high-C wood char (low EC) vs poultry-manure char (high EC, high P/K, high liming) ([same FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).

CEC and nutrient sorption are feedstock- and aging-dependent; [[Biochar in Climate Change Mitigation|reviews biochar climate mitigation]] summarize nutrient and liming co-benefits in the broader CDR/agronomy framing ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00852-8)). Fresh, uninoculated biochar can temporarily **immobilize nutrients**—NRCS explicitly warns that “fresh biochar … has a chance to reduce crop yields by binding and immobilizing nutrients” and recommends compost/manure charging ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf); national standard also: inoculate with compost, compost tea, or manure — [CPS 336 2022 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf) · [[Soil Carbon Amendment Standard|defines NRCS practice 336]]).

### 1.2b Inoculation, charging, and biochar-based fertilizers

Fresh biochar can temporarily **immobilize nutrients**; NRCS and USBI therefore treat **inoculation/charging** as standard practice before agronomic use ([NRCS FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf); [USBI Ag Guidelines](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf) · [[Farm Biochar Application Guidelines|guides farm biochar application]]; CPS 336 inoculate clause: [2022 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf)).

**Three modes** (full chapter [[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]]):

1. **Nutrient charging** — soak/impregnate with NPK solutions, urine, digestate, or manufacture BBFs (pre/post-pyrolysis). [[Biochar-Based Fertilizer Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes biochar fertilizers]] meta: BBFs **+10%** productivity vs fertilized controls ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11104-021-05276-2)). Scoping review of enrichment methods: [Ndoung 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2021.e08473).
2. **Microbial inoculation** — compost tea, PGP/AMF/EM on char carriers; meta shows gains vs biochar alone but weak commercial-field consistency ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/saj2.20792) · [[Microbial Inocula Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes microbial inocula]]). Habitat in raw field-aged char can be sparse ([[Charosphere Microbial Habitat|studies charosphere microbial habitat]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.soilbio.2013.06.004)).
3. **Co-composting (COMBI)** — organic coating + nitrate capture; [[Co-compost Nitrate Capture|shows co-compost nitrate capture]] untreated BC ~**60%** of control biomass vs co-composted up to **~305%** of control ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep11080)); coating chemistry ([Hagemann 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01123-0)); COMBI review ([[COMBI Soil Quality Review|reviews co-composted biochar soil effects]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2020.111443)).

**Practice ranges (sourced):** charge **1–3 weeks** ([USBI](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf)); co-compost roughly **5–15%** biochar w/w early ([Pacific white paper](https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Pacific-Biochar_Biochar-Compost_white-paper.pdf) · [[Biochar Compost White Paper|explains biochar plus compost practice]]; [Antonangelo](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2020.111443)); enriched fertilizers can work at **≪2 t ha⁻¹** root-zone ([[Liquid Nutrient Enrichment Field Trials|liquid nutrient enrichment trials]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761); urine protocol [[Urine-Enhanced Biochar Field Trial|urine-enhanced biochar field trial]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture5030723)).

Credit/MRV: Puro soil amendment **pure/mixed** (AF1–AF2) remains eligible end-use; co-composting does not erase aromatic C stability for permanence chemistry ([Hagemann 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01123-0); [[Puro Biochar Methodology Edition 2025]]). Details: [[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]] §9.

### 1.2c Nutrients & fertilizer chemistry (chapter pointer)

Deep chemistry of N/P/K, CEC/AEC, liming, BBF formulations, and fertilizer-use efficiency: [[Biochar Nutrients and Fertilizer Chemistry]]. Headline metas: soil inorganic N mean ~**−11% NH₄⁺ / −10% NO₃⁻** in the first year class of evidence ([Nguyen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2016.11.004)); BBFs at mean **0.9 t ha⁻¹** give **+10%** productivity vs fertilized controls ([Melo 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11104-021-05276-2)); biochar+fertilizer field synergy ~**+15%** ([[Fertilizer-Controlled Yield Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes yields with fertilizer controls]] · [PDF](https://www.css.cornell.edu/faculty/lehmann/publ/SoilUseManage%2036%2C%202-18%202020%20Ye.pdf)).

### 1.2d Microbes & soil biology (chapter pointer)

Habitat vs sparse colonization, inocula efficacy, AMF/PGPR, priming, and disease-suppression uncertainty: [[Biochar Microbes and Soil Biology]]. Key contrast: biota review ([Lehmann 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.soilbio.2011.04.022)) vs sparse field colonization of wood char ([Quilliam 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.soilbio.2013.06.004)); inocula meta favors local/fungal over commercial products ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/saj2.20792)).

### 1.3 Microbial habitat and SOC interactions

Porous carbon provides habitat and alters redox / sorption environments that shape N cycling (see [[GHG Fluxes]]). For native soil organic carbon:

- [[Stability and Priming Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes stability and priming]] meta-analysis finds a slight, non-significant mean slowdown of SOC mineralization (**−3.8%**, 95% CI −8.1% to +0.8%), with significant **negative** priming in studies under six months (**−8.6%**) and **positive** priming in sandy soils (**+20.8%**) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.12266)).
- Weng et al. (2017) document **decade-scale negative priming** and SOC accrual under biochar in a field context ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate3276)).

NRCS positions biochar as long-lived SOM support versus compost’s shorter labile pulse: FAQ cites research that roughly **60–80%** of biochar C can remain after 100 years versus **2–14%** for compost C, and that ~10 yd³ biochar can match 1-year soil-C increase of ~40 yd³ compost in their comparison framing ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). Cross-check permanence math with [[Permanence]] and [[Biochar Soil GHG Inventory Model|models soil GHG from biochar]] ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).

## 3. GHG fluxes from agricultural soils

Deep dive: [[GHG Fluxes]] · [[Nitrous Oxide Fluxes]] · [[Methane Fluxes]] · [[Priming and SOC Interactions]]. For farm planning:

- **N₂O:** [[Nitrous Oxide Mitigation Meta-analysis|meta-analyzes N2O mitigation]] classic meta ~**−54%** across lab and field studies ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2013.10.009)); Jia et al. (2023) ~**−26.8%** across incubation, pot and field studies ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.4868)). H:C_org of the char is a key moderator ([Cayuela 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2014.12.015)).
- **CH₄:** Mixed; Jia 2023 global mean **−13.0%**; paddies and flooded systems need site-specific reading ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.4868)). Woolf et al. (2021) inventory method assumes **no net CH₄ change** for simplicity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).
- **Inventory conservatism:** Woolf 2021 optional N₂O credit only for applications **>10 Mg C ha⁻¹** in year 1 (illustrative **−23%**), and **not** for organic/forest soils ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).

CDR credit tonnage is dominated by **stable biochar C**, not annual N₂O co-benefits—see [[Biochar as Durable CDR]], [[MRV Stack]].

## 4. Application methods (pointer)

Full practice notes: [[Application Rates and Methods]] · [[Field Rates by System]] · [[Incorporation Topdress Banding]] · [[Equipment and Timing]] · [[On-farm Trial Design]]; blends: [[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]] · [[Co-composting Science]] · [[Farm Co-composting Practice]].

- **Research rates:** often **5–50+ t ha⁻¹** (extremes to 100 t ha⁻¹ in Jeffery 2011) — **not** default farm advice ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015)).
- **Enriched fertilizers:** agronomic doses can be **<2 t ha⁻¹** when nutrient-loaded (Schmidt/Pandit line; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761)).
- **NRCS CPS 336 planning:** FAQ — **4–10 cubic yards per acre** is a good starting range; FY2024 **100% biochar** payment scenario assumes **4 yd³/ac**; blend scenarios specify compost + biochar quantities ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- **Incorporation:** tillage, trenching, no-till drill/prills, manure injectors, fertigation of fines, permanent-bed mixing; surface-only on slopes risks wind/water loss ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- **Pasture:** CPS 336 allowed on pasture; **not** on native rangeland except facilitating Range Planting (550) or Critical Area Planting (342) ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).

## 8. How soil use connects to permanence and credits

1. Produce & test to low H:C_org → [[Properties]], [[Permanence]].
2. Apply to eligible sink (soil / compost / etc.) with chain-of-custody → [[MRV Stack]].
3. Choose methodology (soil modules differ): [[Verra VM0044]], [[Puro Biochar Methodology Edition 2025]], [[Isometric Biochar Protocol]] (soil storage module: https://registry.isometric.com/module/biochar-storage-soil-environments/1.3), [[CSI Global Biochar C-Sink]].
4. Separate **agronomic value** (water, nutrients, yield) from **credit value** (durable C) in farm budgets → [[Agronomic Economics]], [[Market Volumes and Pricing]].


## 9. Decision tree (evidence-based)

Use this as a planning screen—not a substitute for soil tests or NRCS planners.

1. **Soil screen** — Prefer candidates with low OM, coarse texture, or acidic pH (Jeffery 2011/2017; NRCS FAQ). Skip or pilot carefully if alkaline, high-EC, or already high-P with manure-char products ([Jeffery 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015); [Jeffery 2017](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd); [FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
2. **Product screen** — Spec sheet: C_org, H:C_org (<0.7 for CPS 336), ash, EC, liming equivalent, metals, PAHs as required by market/standard ([[Contaminants and Quality]]; [CPS 336 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf)).
3. **Rate screen** — Start near NRCS **4–10 yd³/ac** unless a vineyard redevelopment or research protocol justifies higher (Oasis 10 t/ac; Dominus 25 t/ac) ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf); [Oasis](https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Oasis-Vineyard-Trial_-5th-harvest_-Final-Draft_12.12.2023.pdf); [Dominus](https://pacificbiochar.com/case-study-biochar-application-with-dominus-estate-in-napa-valley-after-2-years-fallow/)).
4. **Blend screen** — Default to compost/manure charge for agronomy; consider co-composting if a local facility pathway exists ([[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]]).
5. **Placement screen** — Band/row for perennials and low doses; broadcast+incorporate for field crops; avoid unprotected surface on HEL slopes ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
6. **Finance screen** — Model yield-only, yield+EQIP, and yield+CDR cases separately ([[Agronomic Economics]]; [Galinato 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2011.07.035)).
7. **MRV screen** — If selling removals: registry choice, application evidence, ownership, no double claim with CPS 336 ([[MRV Stack]], [[Double Counting and Credit Ownership]]).

### Liming arithmetic (NRCS worked example)

If soil test calls for **1 ton lime/ac** and producer applies **1 ton biochar/ac** with liming equivalency **9% CaCO₃**, remaining lime need ≈ **0.91 ton/ac** ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). Using biochar **only** as a lime substitute does **not** meet CPS 336’s stated purposes if that is the sole intent—the FAQ warns against treating liming-as-purpose as 336-eligible framing.

### CEC / nutrient sponge

Biochar’s surface functional groups and porosity can increase effective nutrient retention, but fresh high-temperature wood chars may have relatively low CEC until oxidized/aged or compost-charged. Operational guidance therefore emphasizes inoculation ([CPS 336 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf)). Lehmann et al. 2021 synthesize nutrient and liming co-benefits in the CDR context ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00852-8)).

## 14. Glossary (soil/ag)

- **AWC** — available water capacity (plant-available water between field capacity and permanent wilting point).
- **Charging / inoculation** — loading biochar with nutrients and/or microbes (compost, manure, teas, fertilizers) before or during application.
- **CPS 336** — NRCS Conservation Practice Standard Soil Carbon Amendment.
- **H:C_org** — molar hydrogen to organic carbon ratio; lower ⇒ more aromatic / persistent; NRCS max **0.7**.
- **Liming equivalency** — % CaCO₃ neutralizing value of the amendment.
- **Priming** — change in mineralization rate of native SOC after amendment (positive = faster loss; negative = slower).
- **yd³/ac** — cubic yards per acre (NRCS contracting unit); convert with measured bulk density.

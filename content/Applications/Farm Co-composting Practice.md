# Farm Co-composting Practice

How farms and compost facilities **actually run** biochar co-composting and compost–biochar blends: ratios, timing in the pile, equipment, NRCS payment scenarios, and named facility/field examples. Mechanisms: [[Co-composting Science]]. Safety: [[Compost Blend Safety]]. Charging without a compost facility: [[Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer]]. Rates after the product leaves the pile: [[Field Rates by System]], [[Application Rates and Methods]].

Report context: **2026-09-16**.

---

## 1. Decision tree (practice)

1. **Have an active composting operation?** → Prefer **early co-composting** (COMBI) over mixing finished compost with raw char alone ([[Antonangelo et al. 2021]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2020.111443); [[Pacific Biochar Compost White Paper 2023]]).
2. **No compost facility, but need agronomic response?** → Charge/inoculate **1–3 weeks** with compost, manure, tea, fertilizer, urine, or microbes ([USBI Ag Guidelines](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf) · [[USBI Ag Guidelines 2023]]; NRCS inoculate language [CPS 336 2022 PDF](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf)).
3. **Seeking EQIP/CSP CPS 336?** → Match a published **blend scenario** quantity table and state payment schedule ([NRCS FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf) · [[NRCS CPS 336 FAQ 2026]]; [[NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag]]).
4. **Stacking voluntary CDR?** → Lock ownership / no-double-claim language ([[Matrix Pathways for Credits]], [[Double Counting and Credit Ownership]]).

## 3. Pile management checklist

1. **Char quality gate** before mixing: production >350 °C limited O₂; C_org >10% DW; H:C_org <0.7; metals within CPS 336 Table 3; report ash, liming equivalent, EC, nutrients ([CPS 336 2022](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf); [FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). **No biosolids** feedstocks under CPS 336 (PFAS).
2. **Screen/crush** as needed so particle size matches turning/spreading gear (plugging risk noted in FAQ for drills/injectors).
3. **Add early**, mix thoroughly with wet feedstocks (ABI draft; Pacific white paper).
4. **Moisture / C:N / aeration** — stay in the composting window your facility already uses; Li et al. 2025 recommend C:N **20–30**, moisture **55–65%**, pH **7.5–8.5**, EC **<4 mS cm⁻¹** when optimizing gas mitigation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.48130/nc-0025-0003)).
5. **Cure to maturity** before farm sale or field use — immature compost pathogen/weed risk (NRCS FAQ maturity caution; [[Compost Blend Safety]]).
6. **Document** batch IDs, % biochar (state volume vs weight), feedstock, and lab sheets for both CPS 336 and any CDR offtake ([[Matrix Pathways for Credits]]).

ABI draft economic narrative (qualitative): operators report **shorter processing**, fewer turns (fuel/labor), and higher nutrient value product ([PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-Compost-2026-Draft-V1.pdf)). Rexius case in that draft: **5% by volume** windrow comparison with higher moisture/nitrates/nutrients vs control pile.

## 4. NRCS CPS 336 field blend scenarios (FY2024)

These are **payment scenarios** (acres unit), not universal agronomic optima. From FAQ Table 1 ([PDF](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)):

| Scenario | Compost | Biochar |
|---|---|---|
| 20% biochar / 80% compost | 4.4 tons/ac | 0.8 yd³/ac |
| 40% biochar / 60% compost | 3.3 tons/ac | 1.6 yd³/ac |
| 60% biochar / 40% compost | 2.2 tons/ac | 2.4 yd³/ac |
| 80% biochar / 20% compost | 1.1 tons/ac | 3.2 yd³/ac |
| 100% biochar | — | 4 yd³/ac |
| Compost off-site | 3 tons/ac | — |

Payment rates are **state-specific**. Farmland Info quick guide example: FY2023 typical **60/40** scenario constructed around ~**$160 per cubic yard** average cost components (purchase, shipping, loading, hauling, applying, incorporating)—flat scenario rate, not invoice match ([PDF](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/12/biochar_funding_for_US_producers_quick_guide_.pdf)).

### Manure as inoculant (paid vs unpaid)

NRCS allows mixing biochar with raw manure as inoculant; **CPS 336 does not pay for the manure**—contract the 100% biochar scenario and mix at producer expense under a nutrient management plan; only biochar counts toward minimum quantity ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). Watch **P index** / high-P soils when using manure-rich chars or blends.

Practice lifespan: **1 year**; may be contracted up to **5 times**; ranking improves when stacked with other soil-health practices ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).

## 6. Charging without co-composting (bridge)

If you cannot co-compost: USBI **1–3 week** inoculation/charging window ([PDF](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/06/usbi-biochar-guidelines-for-ag-application.pdf)); urine 1:1 volume ≥1 h protocols ([[Schmidt et al. 2015]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture5030723)); multi-crop liquid enrichment ([[Schmidt et al. 2017]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761)).

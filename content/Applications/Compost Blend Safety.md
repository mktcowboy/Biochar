# Compost Blend Safety

Pathogens, maturity, salts/EC, nutrient imbalance (esp. P), contaminants, and worker/dust risks when using **biochar–compost / manure blends** or co-composted products. Mechanisms: [[Co-composting Science]]. Practice: [[Farm Co-composting Practice]]. Contaminant chemistry of the char itself: [[Contaminants and Quality]] (do not duplicate that chapter). Specs: [[Product Standards EBC IBI]], [[NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag]].

Report context: **2026-09-16**.

---

## 1. Immature compost and pathogens

NRCS CPS 336 FAQ warns to prefer **mature** compost and manage pathogen risk when using compost or manure with biochar ([FAQ PDF](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf) · [[NRCS CPS 336 FAQ 2026]]). Composting itself is used to reduce pathogens, weed seeds, and odor in manure systems—but only when the process reaches adequate time–temperature regimes (Steiner et al. 2010 frame poultry-litter composting that way — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2134/jeq2009.0337)).

**Practice rules of thumb (sourced):**

- Do **not** treat unfinished, cool, anaerobic piles as “inoculated biochar ready for food crops.”
- Document maturity indices your facility already uses (e.g., germination index, NH₄⁺/NO₃⁻ ratio). Maturation meta (2025) reports mean GI **+25.57%**, NH₄⁺-N **−34.96%**, NO₃⁻-N **+32.54%** when biochar is used in composting—useful as process evidence, not a substitute for site testing ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.48130/bchax-0025-0005)).
- Earth Foundries demo: low free ammonium reached **~1 week sooner** with 10% v/v biochar ([PDF](https://www.earthfoundriesinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Earth-Foundries-Green-Compost-Biochar-Composting-Project-Report-2023.pdf) · [[Earth Foundries Co-Composting Report 2023]]).

## 2. Salts and electrical conductivity (EC)

| Guidance | Detail | URL |
|---|---|---|
| NRCS FAQ | Biochar can intensify salinization at high rates; look for spec-sheet **EC under 8** (FAQ guidance); manure-based biochars are higher in salts | https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf |
| NRCS FAQ examples | High-C wood char typically lower EC; poultry-manure char example extremely high in salts and nutrients—may suit very acidic, low-nutrient, high-rainfall soils; **not** neutral-to-basic soils with high P or salts | same FAQ |
| Earth Foundries finished compost | Ca and Mg salts down by **more than half** vs control pile in that demo | https://www.earthfoundriesinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Earth-Foundries-Green-Compost-Biochar-Composting-Project-Report-2023.pdf |
| Li et al. 2025 composting meta | High EC (**>4 mS cm⁻¹**) impaired mitigation effects of biochar on target gases | https://doi.org/10.48130/nc-0025-0003 |

Soil test **EC** before and after high-rate manure-char or manure-blend applications. Pair with [[Field Rates by System]].

## 3. Phosphorus, nutrient imbalance, and manure

- NRCS FAQ discusses **P-index** / high-P soils when using manure-rich chars or blends; Northeast P-index discussion in FAQ ([PDF](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Manure used only as **inoculant** under CPS 336 is **unpaid**; keep it under a nutrient management plan ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Fresh wood char can **immobilize** nutrients and cut yields if applied raw (NRCS FAQ; Kammann untreated 2% w/w → ~60% of control biomass — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep11080)). Blends and co-composting are risk-management, not a license to skip soil tests ([[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]]).

## 4. Feedstock and contaminant gates

CPS 336 / FAQ:

- **No biosolids** or municipal-waste feedstocks under CPS 336 (PFAS concerns) ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf); [2022 standard](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/336-NHCP-CPS-Soil-Carbon-Amendment-2022.pdf)).
- Metals within Table 3 maxima; H:C_org <0.7; C_org >10% DW; production >350 °C limited oxygen.
- IBI seal alone is **not** sufficient without a recent **spec sheet** (FAQ).
- Residues that would normally stay on the land cannot be used as feedstock (FAQ).

Deep PAH/metals/PFAS discussion: [[Contaminants and Quality]]. Product labels: [[Product Standards EBC IBI]] · [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]].

## 5. Alkalinity / liming interactions

NRCS: biochar is usually alkaline; most useful as liming on acid soils; on soils already **pH >7**, check liming equivalency and add cautiously. Worked example: 1 t/ac biochar at **9% CaCO₃** equivalency offsets ~9% of a 1-ton lime recommendation ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)). Compost blends change the liming arithmetic—use lab sheets for **both** components.

## 6. Dust, handling, and placement safety

- Surface-only applications on slopes risk wind/water loss of fine char (NRCS FAQ incorporation guidance — [PDF](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Moist blends and co-composted products generally dust less than dry fines; still use PPE appropriate to lime/manure spreading norms.
- Placement methods and equipment: [[Incorporation Topdress Banding]], [[Equipment and Timing]].

---

## 7. Credit / claim hygiene (safety of claims)

Do **not** claim soil N₂O reductions from facility VOC/NH₃ data. Earth Foundries **VOC −33%** is a **composting-process** metric ([[Earth Foundries Co-Composting Report 2023]]); soil flux evidence lives in [[GHG Fluxes]]. Do not double-claim the same tonne as CPS 336 cost-share and voluntary CDR without contractual separation ([[Matrix Pathways for Credits]], [[Double Counting and Credit Ownership]]).

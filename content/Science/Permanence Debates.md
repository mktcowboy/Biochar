# Permanence Debates

Chapter on **open scientific and policy debates** about biochar permanence: decay-model form, inertinite/Ro claims, horizon choice, and what “permanent” should mean for CDR accounting. Complements the settled mechanism snapshot in [[Permanence]] and the proxy chapters. Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Consensus vs Debates]] · [[Permanence Science for Credits]] · [[Reflectance Ro and HyPy]] · [[Woolf IPCC Fperm]] · [[Risks and Controversies]] · [[Science Hub]] · [[Key Sources]]

---

## 1. What is largely settled

- Well-made biochar (adequate T / low H/C_org) persists **far longer** than raw biomass; fused aromatics are the mechanistic basis ([[Lehmann et al. 2021]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00852-8)).
- Persistence is **probabilistic** and environment-dependent, not literal immortality ([Schmidt et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092); [[Woolf et al. 2021]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)).
- Inventory practice can use mass × \(F_C\) × \(F_\mathrm{perm}\) with kiln/gate MRV ([[CDR Potential and IPCC Accounting]]).

## 2. Debate A — Do short incubations overestimate long-term decay?

**Claim:** Two-pool fits from incubations lasting only a few years may **mis-estimate** (often **overestimate**) millennial decay of the recalcitrant pool, because the “slow” pool is still a mixture and true inert fractions need other assays (HyPy, Ro, conductivity) (frontier discussion in science report 2026-09-14; Azzi et al. 2024 modelling work listed in [[Key Sources]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geoderma.2023.116761)).

**Counterweight:** Woolf/IPCC deliberately use ≥1-year multi-pool fits and conservative exclusions (no priming credit, mineral soils only) so inventory factors are less likely to **over-credit** ([Woolf 2021](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425); [IPCC PDF](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2019rf/pdf/4_Volume4/19R_V4_Ch02_Ap4_Biochar.pdf)).

**Vault posture:** Active research—not settled. Prefer transparent horizon + method; cross-check high-permanence marketing claims against [[Reflectance Ro and HyPy]] and Schmidt policy commentary.

## 3. Debate B — Decomposition-model camp vs inertinite/Ro camp

| Camp | Core tools | Typical horizons | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decomposition-model** | Multi-pool decay, H/C_org, T-class, soil T | 100y (IPCC/Woolf); “several centuries” / 200y in some markets | [[IPCC 2019 Biochar Appendix]], [[Woolf et al. 2021]], Puro CORC200+, Isometric 200y |
| **Inertinite / Ro** | Random reflectance, HyPy-resistant C | Millennial / “permanent” upper classes | [[Sanei et al. 2024]], Isometric 1000y, CSI upper class |

[[Sanei et al. 2024]] IBRo2% (Ro = 2%) and modelled ~100 Myr half-life under stated oxidizing conditions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409)) energize the Ro camp. [Schmidt et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092) push back: do not equate modern biochar with geological inertinite; refuse **100%** permanence declarations; recommend policy definitions on the order of **centuries** with registered material properties, traceable application, conservative modelling, and long-term field validation.

Puro’s public permanence post (2025-07-03) explicitly chose **centuries not millennia** pending more integrated science ([puro.earth permanence post](https://puro.earth/insights/post/biochar-permanence/))—a market illustration of Debate B, detailed on the credit side in [[Permanence Science for Credits]] / [[Puro CORC200+]].

## 4. Debate C — Which time horizon is “the” permanence number?

UNFCCC GWP practice and IPCC biochar appendix center **100 years** ([IPCC PDF](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2019rf/pdf/4_Volume4/19R_V4_Ch02_Ap4_Biochar.pdf); Woolf discussion of GWP analogy [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425)). Woolf also publishes 500- and 1000-year factors showing large further losses (e.g. high-T at 14.9 °C: 0.82 → 0.25 from 100y to 1000y — [Table 3](https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content)).

Choosing 100y vs 1000y changes both the **number** and the **narrative**. Neither is wrong if labeled; mixing them in marketing is.

## 5. Debate D — Climate feedback and non-soil uses

- **Warming feedback:** higher future soil T lowers \(F_\mathrm{perm}\) ([[Soil Temperature and Environment]]).
- **Non-soil matrices** (concrete, asphalt, polymers): oxygen/microbe exposure differs; soil \(F_\mathrm{perm}\) tables do not automatically transfer ([[Materials and Built Uses]], [[Consensus vs Debates]]).

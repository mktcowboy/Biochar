# Reflectance Ro and HyPy

Chapter on **optical random reflectance (Ro)** and **hydropyrolysis-resistant carbon (BC_HyPy)** as advanced permanence proxies beyond elemental ratios and pyrolysis-temperature class. Positions the “inertinite / Ro camp” relative to Woolf-style decay models without duplicating credit rules ([[Permanence Science for Credits]]). Report context: **2026-09-16**.

**Related notes:** [[Permanence]] · [[H-Corg and O-Corg]] · [[Woolf IPCC Fperm]] · [[Permanence Debates]] · [[Sanei et al. 2024]] · [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] · [[CSI Global Biochar C-Sink]] · [[Science Hub]] · [[Key Sources]]

## 1. Why advanced proxies exist

Elemental ratios and T-class \(F_\mathrm{perm}\) are accessible but imperfect: short incubations may mis-estimate millennial recalcitrant pools, and H/C_org regressions leave substantial unexplained variance (Woolf 100-yr H/C \(R^2 \approx 0.3\) at 14.9 °C — [open PDF](https://open-science.canada.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/112fc297-0352-4683-ae0e-dd1271f59d77/content) Table 3). Petrography and selective chemical oxidation/hydropyrolysis aim to isolate the **most condensed** carbon fraction—analogous to inertinite in coal geology ([[Sanei et al. 2024]] · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409); policy framing in [Schmidt et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092)).

## 2. Random reflectance (Ro)

### 2.1 What it measures

**Random reflectance** is the percentage of incident light reflected from polished carbonaceous particles under oil immersion—standard coal petrography (ISO 7404-5). Higher Ro tracks higher aromatic condensation / thermal rank. [[ANSI ASABE USBI S668]] §10.3 specifies Ro per **ISO 7404-5** (white-light microscopy) as a permanence assessment method ([PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf))—methods only, not pass/fail.

### 2.2 Sanei et al. 2024 inertinite benchmark

[[Sanei et al. 2024]] propose an **inertinite benchmark** at random reflectance **Ro = 2%** (“IBRo2%”) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409); abstract checked).

| Claim (abstract) | Value |
|---|---|
| Benchmark | **Ro = 2%** |
| Commercial samples | **76%** had their **entire** Ro distribution above 2% |
| Modelled half-life | Order of **~100 million years** for typical inertinite biochar under highly oxidizing conditions at constant **30 °C** |
| Other pools | Incomplete carbonization may leave up to three less-stable pools (geochemical pyrolysis + Ro) |
| Process inference | Ro can back-calculate peak particle temperatures |

**Use with care (vault source note):** the multi-Myr half-life is a **kinetic model for inertinite under stated conditions**, not a field measurement of soil biochar decay ([[Sanei et al. 2024]]).

### 2.3 Policy / science tension

[Schmidt et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092) argue biochars and inertinite-ranked fossil coals **must not be equated**: geological inertinite experienced burial, mineral inclusion, high P–T over geologic time; modern pyrolysis chars are different materials. Field and lab studies show **measurable though small** mineralization across biochar types; declaring **100%** permanence over millennia is inconsistent with current understanding. Analytical proxies indicate **relative**, not absolute, persistence.

Credit programs that use Ro (e.g. Isometric 1000y pathway, CSI upper class) make **standards decisions**—see [[Permanence Science for Credits]], [[Isometric Permanence Pathways 200y and 1000y]], [[CSI Global Biochar C-Sink]]—not automatic science conclusions.

## 3. Hydropyrolysis-resistant carbon (BC_HyPy)

**Hydropyrolysis (HyPy)** subjects organic matter to high-pressure hydrogen with a catalyst, destroying labile organic carbon while leaving a refractory carbon residue used as a black-carbon / pyrogenic-carbon assay in geochemistry. In biochar MRV discussions, the **HyPy-resistant fraction (BC_HyPy)** is treated as a high-permanence pool proxy (science report / Schmidt multi-proxy discussion; Woolf 2021 lists HyPy among emerging proxies in the broader permanence literature cited by later commentaries).

Practical points for this vault:

- BC_HyPy is a **material** metric (fraction resistant to HyPy), complementary to H/C_org and Ro.
- CSI Global Biochar C-Sink documentation discusses **HyPy ≥90%** (with Ro thresholds) for upper permanence classes—**thresholds belong in** [[CSI Global Biochar C-Sink]] / [[Permanence Science for Credits]], not restated as science universal constants here.
- Pair HyPy with elemental ratios and process data; do not treat any single proxy as absolute millennial proof ([Schmidt 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092)).

Method standardization for U.S. labs remains thinner than for H:C_org; S668 currently lists H:C_org, O:C_org, and Ro explicitly in §10 ([PDF](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf)).

## 4. Solid electrical conductivity (brief)

EBC lists **electrical conductivity of the solid biochar** (not leachate EC) as an important indirect parameter for batch homogeneity and as a correlate of certain use effects; it is part of the EBC physical package ([EBC 10.5E](https://www.european-biochar.org/media/doc/7/ebc_en_10_5_red.pdf) §7.8). Some permanence commentaries group solid EC with HyPy/Ro as multi-proxy toolkit entries ([Schmidt 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092)). It is **not** a substitute for \(F_\mathrm{perm}\) tables.

## 5. How to read Ro/HyPy next to Woolf \(F_\mathrm{perm}\)

| Approach | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Woolf / IPCC multi-pool decay | Empirically tied to mineralization data; T- and H/C-resolved | Extrapolates beyond incubation length; \(R^2\) modest |
| Ro inertinite benchmark | Fast optical screen; ranks condensation | Geological analogy contested; model half-lives ≠ field soil |
| BC_HyPy | Chemically isolates refractory C | Lab capacity; mapping to \(F_\mathrm{perm}(t)\) still protocol-specific |

Synthesis for this vault: use **Ro/HyPy to document high condensation** and to satisfy protocols that require them; use **Woolf/IPCC** when you need an explicit remaining-fraction vs time and soil T. Holding both without claiming identity is the conservative scientific posture ([[Permanence Debates]]).

---

## 6. Practice takeaway

1. If a buyer or methodology asks for Ro, specify **ISO 7404-5** and report the **distribution**, not only a mean ([S668](https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/ANSI-S668.pdf); [Sanei 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coal.2023.104409)).
2. Never equate “Ro ≥ 2%” with “zero decay forever” ([Schmidt 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcbb.70092)).
3. For inventory-style tonnes CO₂e, still need \(F_C \times F_\mathrm{perm}\) logic ([[Woolf IPCC Fperm]]) unless the governing protocol replaces it.

# CDR Potential and IPCC Accounting

## IPCC inventory method

IPCC *2019 Refinement* Vol. 4 Appendix 4 provides a method for estimating biochar C in mineral cropland/grassland soils ([PDF](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2019rf/pdf/4_Volume4/19R_V4_Ch02_Ap4_Biochar.pdf)).

**F_perm after 100 years** (conservative ~20 °C basis in appendix notes):

| Temperature class | F_perm |
|---|---|
| Low (350–450 °C) | **0.65** |
| Medium (450–600 °C) | **0.80** |
| High (>600 °C / high-T gasification) | **0.89** |

Uncertainty roughly ±11–15%. Guidance / basis for future Tier 1—**not** a credit methodology. See [[Verra VM0044]], [[Puro CORC200+]], etc. for market methods.

Woolf et al. (2021) extends this with H/C_org and soil-temperature predictors. DOI: [10.1021/acs.est.1c02425](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.1c02425).

## Mitigation / CDR potential estimates

| Source | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Woolf et al. 2010 | Max **1.8 Pg CO₂-Cₑ yr⁻¹** (≈ **~6.6 Pg CO₂e** if ×44/12); century total **130 Pg CO₂-Cₑ**. Prefer Lehmann 2021 / Weng 2025 for updated framing. |
| Lehmann et al. 2021 | Emission reductions **3.4–6.3 Pg CO₂e yr⁻¹** (~half CDR); residues-only **2.4–3.9**; with land sparing + CCS up to **~7.4–10.3** |
| Weng & Cowie 2025 | Literature **0.03–11**; credible multi-Gt scale |
| State of CDR 2026 (Fuss et al.) | Sustainable removal **≤~2.7 GtCO₂ yr⁻¹**; total mitigation **≤~10.3**; IAM 2050 **<0.1–1.1** |
| Costs | Often **~$70–360/tCO₂** (recent); broader historical **~$10–345** |

## LCA / system boundaries

Net climate benefit is **dominated by biochar C permanence vs baseline biomass fate**, modulated by methane avoidance (rice, landfill), energy co-products, transport, and kiln CH₄ if poorly engineered (Roberts 2010 onward; Matuštík et al. 2020 review; Lehmann 2021).

State of CDR (2026) and Weng & Cowie (2025) place biochar as **largest current novel CDR contributor** with gigaton-scale technical/sustainable potential but much lower near-term economic/IAM deployment.

## MRV accounting insight (science side)

MRV can focus on mass produced × C fraction × permanence factor at the kiln/gate, with application verification—cheaper than full soil flux monitoring (Lehmann 2021). Markets implement this differently—see [[MRV Stack]].

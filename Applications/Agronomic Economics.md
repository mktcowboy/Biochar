# Agronomic Economics

Sourced cost and benefit figures only. Biochar farm NPV is sensitive to **delivered $/ton**, **application rate**, **yield response**, **crop price**, and whether **carbon finance** or **NRCS cost-share** is stacked. Do not treat any single trial as a universal ROI.

Related: [[Crop Yield Effects]] · [[Real World Soil Projects]] · [[NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag]] · [[Market Volumes and Pricing]].

## Headline insight from peer-reviewed economics

Galinato, Yoder & Granatstein (2011), *Energy Policy* — “The economic value of biochar in crop production and carbon sequestration” ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2011.07.035); ScienceDirect: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421511005672):

- Evaluates biochar for **Washington winter wheat** with and without carbon payments.
- Finds profitability generally requires **low biochar prices** and/or a **carbon offset market**; agronomic value alone is often insufficient at then-prevailing prices and the high application rates modeled.
- Use as a caution against assuming fertilizer-like payback from bulk raw char in temperate systems—consistent with weak temperate mean yields in [Jeffery et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd).

Exact break-even tables are in the paper; quote numbers from the PDF/HTML when doing diligence rather than secondary paraphrases.

## Multi-year vineyard field economics (California)

### Oasis Vineyard Trial (Monterey County) — primary PDF

Source: https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Oasis-Vineyard-Trial_-5th-harvest_-Final-Draft_12.12.2023.pdf

| Item | Value (as reported) |
|---|---|
| Biochar delivered price (2016, wet) | **$240 / ton** |
| Compost delivered price (2016, wet) | **$40 / ton** |
| Grape price used (Oct 2023) | ~**$1,500 / ton** |
| Rates | Biochar **10 t/ac**; compost **15 t/ac**; combo both |
| Extra revenue by 5th harvest vs control | Biochar **$9,752/ac**; compost **$12,569/ac**; combo **$14,937/ac** |
| Rough profit after amendment cost | Biochar **$7,352/ac**; compost **$11,969/ac**; combo **$11,937/ac** |
| Payback | Positive ROI by **second harvest** for all treatments |
| Illustrative extrapolation (biochar alone) | Authors’ scenario: if next 5 years at half observed gain → ~**$12,228/ac** over 10 yr (~5× biochar investment); 20-harvest stretch ~**$21,980/ac** |

**Caveats stated in report:** revenue highly dependent on grape price; same irrigation/fertilizer cost across treatments (so water/fertilizer savings not monetized); woody high-T char persistence cited via Woolf et al. 2021.

## US cost-share (NRCS CPS 336)

- Practice is **financial assistance** (EQIP / related), **not** a carbon credit ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Scenario payment example (Farmland Info guide): FY2023 **60/40 biochar/compost** constructed ~**$160 / cubic yard** covering average purchase + logistics + application components; **varies by state**; not matched to actual invoices ([PDF](https://farmlandinfo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/12/biochar_funding_for_US_producers_quick_guide_.pdf)).
- Ranking: 336 alone often ranks low; stack with other CSAF soil-health practices ([FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Double counting with voluntary CDR: [[Double Counting and Credit Ownership]].

## Decision framework (sourced logic)

1. **Estimate agronomic delta** from climate/soil analogues ([[Crop Yield Effects]] — tropics/acid/coarse stronger).
2. **Price the amendment** delivered + incorporation (Oasis and NRCS scenario docs as templates).
3. **Add policy stack:** CPS 336 cost-share and/or voluntary CDR offtake—with legal separation of claims.
4. **Stress-test** crop price and zero-yield-response cases (Galinato caution for temperate wheat systems).
5. Prefer **blends / enriched fertilizers / banding** when aiming to cut rate and cost ([[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]], Nepal low-dose evidence https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761).

# Crop Yield Effects

Yield responses to biochar are **context-specific**—stronger in acid, coarse-textured, degraded, and **tropical** soils; weaker or null on average in many **temperate fertile** systems. Biochar is **not** a universal fertilizer substitute. Pair with [[Soil and Agriculture]], [[Application Rates and Methods]], [[Biochar Compost and Manure Blends]], and [[Agronomic Economics]].

## Meta-analytic estimates

| Source | Design | Effect | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jeffery et al. (2011) *Agric. Ecosyst. Environ.* | Meta of productivity (yield or aboveground biomass) | Grand mean **~+10%**; subgroup means roughly **−28% to +39%** | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015 |
| Jeffery et al. (2017) *Environ. Res. Lett.* 12:053001 | Tropical vs temperate | Tropical mean ~**+25%**; temperate mean little/no benefit | https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd |
| Ye et al. (2020) *Soil Use Manage.* 36:2–18 | Field studies; separate fertilizer controls | Biochar **+ inorganic fertilizer** ~**+15%** vs fertilizer alone; biochar alone often weak | https://www.css.cornell.edu/faculty/lehmann/publ/SoilUseManage%2036%2C%202-18%202020%20Ye.pdf |
| Lehmann et al. (2021) *Nat. Geosci.* | Synthesis citing Ye and related field work | Fertilised field contexts can show ~**+15%**-class responses **not** 1:1 correlated with fertilizer response | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00852-8 |
| Jia et al. (2023) *Land Degrad. Dev.* | Global patterns GHG + crop yield | Crop yield **+9.4%** overall | https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.4868 |

### Mechanistic subgroups (Jeffery 2011)

From the 2011 meta ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agee.2011.08.015)):

- **Acidic soils ~+14%**, neutral ~+13%; liming hypothesized as a major pathway.
- **Coarse ~+10%**, medium ~+13% textures; water holding implicated.
- **100 t ha⁻¹** subgroup showed the largest positive mean (**+39%**) — research extreme, not a farm recommendation.
- **Poultry-litter** feedstock subgroup strong positive (~**+28%**); **biosolids** feedstock the only significant **negative** feedstock mean (~**−28%**).

### Tropical vs temperate (Jeffery 2017)

Jeffery et al. (2017) conclude biochar **boosts tropical but not temperate** mean crop yields, attributing tropical gains largely to liming and nutrient effects in acidic, nutrient-poor soils ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd); open PDF mirror: https://ecoss.nau.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Jeffery_2017_Environ._Res._Lett._12_053001.pdf).

## Field trials and real deployments (selected)

Details and more URLs: [[Real World Soil Projects]].

### Oasis Vineyard (Monterey County, CA) — multi-year

Pre-plant treatments on Pinot Noir (Oceano Sand / Garey Sandy Loam, ~0.7% OM): control; compost **15 t/ac**; biochar **10 t/ac**; compost+biochar. Same irrigation/fertilizer regime. Across **five harvests**, average yield increases vs control: compost+biochar **+36%**, biochar **+25%**, compost **+29%** (combined five-season totals 41.7 / 38.2 / 40.1 vs 31.7 t/ac). Quality not significantly degraded. Primary report: https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Oasis-Vineyard-Trial_-5th-harvest_-Final-Draft_12.12.2023.pdf

### Exomad Green field study (Santa Cruz, Bolivia) — 2025 reporting

Company/trade reporting of trials under drought: corn **+15%** biochar alone, up to **+32%** with fertilizer + microorganisms; beans **+13%** alone, up to **+25%** combined ([Bioenergy International 2025-07-04](https://bioenergyinternational.com/biochar-field-study-reveals-significant-crop-yield-improvements-in-bolivia/)). Aligns with tropical/acid-soil expectation from Jeffery 2017. Facility/soil end-use: [Puro Exomad Concepción](https://puro.earth/cdr-credit-suppliers/facilities/exomad-green-concepcion/).

### Nepal — enriched biochar fertilizers (low dose)

- Schmidt et al. (2015): urine-enhanced biochar + compost produced very large pumpkin yield responses relative to urine or biochar alone on a fertile tropical soil ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture5030723)).
- Pandit / Schmidt et al.: **21 field trials**, **13 crop species**, liquid nutrient–enriched biochar fertilizers ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761)).

These support the [[Soil and Agriculture]] point that **charged / biochar-based fertilizers** can work at **≪ bulk research rates**.

### Brazil — degraded pastures

Latawiec et al. (2019) evaluate biochar on degraded pasturelands with environmental and cost-benefit analysis ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-47647-x); PDF: https://www.iis-rio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Latawiec_et_al-2019-Scientific_Reports.pdf).

## When yields fail to rise

- Temperate, already-fertile, near-neutral pH soils ([Jeffery 2017](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa67bd)).
- Fresh, high-sorption char without compost/manure charge ([NRCS FAQ](https://nrcs-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/2026-04/soil-carbon-amendment-ac-336-faq.pdf)).
- Contaminated or inappropriate feedstocks (biosolids subgroup negative in Jeffery 2011; PFAS → biosolids **excluded** under CPS 336).
- Alkaline soils with high-liming chars (FAQ).
- Economics: even positive yield deltas may not repay purchase price without carbon finance — [[Agronomic Economics]], [Galinato et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2011.07.035).

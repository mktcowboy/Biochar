Crop Yield Effects

Yield responses to biochar are context-specific—stronger in acid, coarse-textured, degraded, and tropical soils; weaker or null on average in many temperate fertile systems. Biochar is not a universal fertilizer substitute. Pair with Soil and Agriculture, Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer, Application Rates and Methods, Biochar Compost and Manure Blends, and Agronomic Economics.

Mechanistic subgroups (Jeffery 2011)

From the 2011 meta (DOI):

  • Acidic soils ~+14%, neutral ~+13%; liming hypothesized as a major pathway.
  • Coarse ~+10%, medium ~+13% textures; water holding implicated.
  • 100 t ha⁻¹ subgroup showed the largest positive mean (+39%) — research extreme, not a farm recommendation.
  • Poultry-litter feedstock subgroup strong positive (+28%); biosolids feedstock the only significant negative feedstock mean (−28%).

Tropical vs temperate (Jeffery 2017)

Jeffery et al. (2017) conclude biochar boosts tropical but not temperate mean crop yields, attributing tropical gains largely to liming and nutrient effects in acidic, nutrient-poor soils (DOI; open PDF mirror: https://ecoss.nau.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/Jeffery_2017_Environ._Res._Lett._12_053001.pdf).

Field trials and real deployments (selected)

Details and more URLs: Real World Soil Projects.

Oasis Vineyard (Monterey County, CA) — multi-year

Pre-plant treatments on Pinot Noir (Oceano Sand / Garey Sandy Loam, ~0.7% OM): control; compost 15 t/ac; biochar 10 t/ac; compost+biochar. Same irrigation/fertilizer regime. Across five harvests, average yield increases vs control: compost+biochar +36%, biochar +25%, compost +29% (combined five-season totals 41.7 / 38.2 / 40.1 vs 31.7 t/ac). Quality not significantly degraded. Primary report: https://pacificbiochar.com/wp-content/uploads/Oasis-Vineyard-Trial_-5th-harvest_-Final-Draft_12.12.2023.pdf

Exomad Green field study (Santa Cruz, Bolivia) — 2025 reporting

Company/trade reporting of trials under drought: corn +15% biochar alone, up to +32% with fertilizer + microorganisms; beans +13% alone, up to +25% combined (Bioenergy International 2025-07-04). Aligns with tropical/acid-soil expectation from Jeffery 2017. Facility/soil end-use: Puro Exomad Concepción.

Nepal — enriched biochar fertilizers (low dose)

  • Schmidt et al. (2015): urine-enhanced biochar + compost produced very large pumpkin yield responses relative to urine or biochar alone on a fertile tropical soil (DOI).
  • Pandit / Schmidt et al.: 21 field trials, 13 crop species, liquid nutrient–enriched biochar fertilizers (DOI).

These support the Soil and Agriculture point that charged / biochar-based fertilizers can work at ≪ bulk research rates.

Brazil — degraded pastures

Latawiec et al. (2019) evaluate biochar on degraded pasturelands with environmental and cost-benefit analysis (DOI; PDF: https://www.iis-rio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Latawiec_et_al-2019-Scientific_Reports.pdf).

When yields fail to rise

  • Temperate, already-fertile, near-neutral pH soils (Jeffery 2017).
  • Fresh, high-sorption char without compost/manure charge (NRCS FAQ).
  • Contaminated or inappropriate feedstocks (biosolids subgroup negative in Jeffery 2011; PFAS → biosolids excluded under CPS 336).
  • Alkaline soils with high-liming chars (FAQ).
  • Economics: even positive yield deltas may not repay purchase price without carbon finance — Agronomic Economics, Galinato et al. 2011.

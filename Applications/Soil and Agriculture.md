# Soil and Agriculture

Hub for farm and soil use of biochar: agronomic effects, greenhouse-gas fluxes, US practice standards, and how soil placement connects to permanence science and credits.

## Purpose

Most historical research and much of today’s credit volume assume **soil application**. Outcomes are highly context-specific (soil type, climate, rate, feedstock/process). This note does not invent new field results—it maps existing vault notes and marks research gaps.

## How the pieces fit

1. **Production & properties** set liming, porosity, nutrients, and contaminant risk → [[Production Hub]].
2. **Field effects** (yields, GHG) are empirical and variable → [[Crop Yield Effects]], [[GHG Fluxes]].
3. **Permanence** science underpins how much applied C is treated as durable storage → [[Permanence]].
4. **Policy / incentives** in the US include NRCS CPS 336 (practice standard, not a credit methodology) → [[NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag]].
5. **Credits** add MRV and ownership rules on top of soil placement → [[MRV Stack]], [[Carbon Markets Hub]].

## Gaps / to research

- Soil-type and climate decision trees for when biochar is agronomically justified
- Interaction of biochar with fertilizer, manure, and cover crops
- Long-term field monitoring designs that satisfy both agronomy and credit MRV
- Farmer economics (cost per hectare vs yield/GHG co-benefits) — source before writing
- Coexistence of NRCS payments and voluntary credit claims (see also [[Double Counting and Credit Ownership]])

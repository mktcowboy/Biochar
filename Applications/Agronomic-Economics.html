Agronomic Economics

Chapter on farm-level cost and benefit of soil-applied biochar and blends. Sourced figures only. NPV is sensitive to delivered $/ton, application rate, yield response, crop price, and whether carbon finance or NRCS cost-share is stacked. Do not treat any single trial as a universal ROI.

Report context: 2026-09-16.

Sibling notes: Cost Components · Fertilizer Displacement · Field Rates by System · Crop Yield Effects · Real World Soil Projects · Matrix Pathways for Credits · NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag · Market Volumes and Pricing.

1. Headline insight from peer-reviewed economics

Galinato, Yoder & Granatstein (2011), Energy Policy — “The economic value of biochar in crop production and carbon sequestration” (DOI; ScienceDirect: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421511005672):

  • Evaluates biochar for Washington winter wheat with and without carbon payments.
  • Finds profitability generally requires low biochar prices and/or a carbon offset market; agronomic value alone is often insufficient at then-prevailing prices and the high application rates modeled.
  • Use as a caution against assuming fertilizer-like payback from bulk raw char in temperate systems—consistent with weak temperate mean yields in Jeffery et al. 2017 (DOI).

Exact break-even tables are in the paper; quote numbers from the PDF/HTML when doing diligence rather than secondary paraphrases. This vault does not invent those table cells without full-text check.

3. US cost-share (NRCS CPS 336)

  • Practice is financial assistance (EQIP / related), not a carbon credit — see Double Counting and Credit Ownership. FAQ does not treat CPS 336 as a credit methodology (FAQ PDF · NRCS CPS 336 FAQ 2026).
  • Scenario payment example (Farmland Info guide): FY2023 60/40 biochar/compost constructed ~$160 / cubic yard covering average purchase + logistics + application components; varies by state; not matched to actual invoices (PDF). Line-item framing: Cost Components.
  • Ranking: 336 alone often ranks low; stack with other CSAF soil-health practices (FAQ).
  • Lifespan 1 year, up to 5 contracts (FAQ).
  • Funding guides: ABI 2026 https://biochar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Biochar-Guidelines-Funding-2026-final.pdf · NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag.

7. Decision framework (sourced logic)

  1. Estimate agronomic delta from climate/soil analogues (Crop Yield Effects — tropics/acid/coarse stronger).
  2. Price the amendment delivered + incorporation (Cost Components; Oasis and NRCS scenario docs as templates).
  3. Add policy stack: CPS 336 cost-share and/or voluntary CDR offtake—with legal separation of claims.
  4. Stress-test crop price and zero-yield-response cases (Galinato caution for temperate wheat systems).
  5. Prefer blends / enriched fertilizers / banding when aiming to cut rate and cost (Biochar Compost and Manure Blends, Nepal low-dose evidence https://doi.org/10.1002/ldr.2761 · Schmidt et al. 2017).
  6. Run a local strip trial before scaling (On-farm Trial Design).

  1. Amendment cost/ac ≈ (10 t biochar × 240)+(optional15tcompost×40) = 2,400∗∗biochar−onlyor∗∗3,000 combo materials at 2016 delivered wet prices (Oasis PDF).
  2. Cumulative extra revenue by harvest 5 at ~1,500/tgrapes:biochar∗∗9,752/ac**, combo $14,937/ac (same PDF).
  3. Authors’ rough profit after amendment cost: biochar 7,352/ac∗∗,combo∗∗11,937/ac (same PDF).
  4. Stress tests the report itself flags: grape price; no water/fertilizer savings counted; persistence assumed via Woolf for high-T wood char.

For NRCS-offset scenarios, subtract expected CPS 336 payment (state schedule) from net cost—using Farmland Info’s ~$160/yd³ only as a scenario construction example, not your invoice (PDF).

10. Linking to credit prices without double counting

CDR market prices (Market Volumes and Pricing, https://www.cdr.fyi/) can close Galinato-style gaps only if:

  1. The tonne is eligible under a methodology soil pathway (Matrix Pathways for Credits),
  2. Ownership is clear if CPS 336 also paid (Double Counting and Credit Ownership),
  3. You do not count flux co-benefits twice (GHG Fluxes).

Literature removal cost bands (~$70–360/tCO₂ — Fuss & Lunstrum 2026 · DOI) are not credit bid/ask.

Farm Co-composting Practice

How farms and compost facilities actually run biochar co-composting and compost–biochar blends: ratios, timing in the pile, equipment, NRCS payment scenarios, and named facility/field examples. Mechanisms: Co-composting Science. Safety: Compost Blend Safety. Charging without a compost facility: Biochar Inoculation for Fertilizer. Rates after the product leaves the pile: Field Rates by System, Application Rates and Methods.

Report context: 2026-09-16.

1. Decision tree (practice)

  1. Have an active composting operation? → Prefer early co-composting (COMBI) over mixing finished compost with raw char alone (Antonangelo et al. 2021 · DOI; Pacific Biochar Compost White Paper 2023).
  2. No compost facility, but need agronomic response? → Charge/inoculate 1–3 weeks with compost, manure, tea, fertilizer, urine, or microbes (USBI Ag Guidelines · USBI Ag Guidelines 2023; NRCS inoculate language CPS 336 2022 PDF).
  3. Seeking EQIP/CSP CPS 336? → Match a published blend scenario quantity table and state payment schedule (NRCS FAQ · NRCS CPS 336 FAQ 2026; NRCS CPS 336 and US Ag).
  4. Stacking voluntary CDR? → Lock ownership / no-double-claim language (Matrix Pathways for Credits, Double Counting and Credit Ownership).

3. Pile management checklist

  1. Char quality gate before mixing: production >350 °C limited O₂; C_org >10% DW; H:C_org <0.7; metals within CPS 336 Table 3; report ash, liming equivalent, EC, nutrients (CPS 336 2022; FAQ). No biosolids feedstocks under CPS 336 (PFAS).
  2. Screen/crush as needed so particle size matches turning/spreading gear (plugging risk noted in FAQ for drills/injectors).
  3. Add early, mix thoroughly with wet feedstocks (ABI draft; Pacific white paper).
  4. Moisture / C:N / aeration — stay in the composting window your facility already uses; Li et al. 2025 recommend C:N 20–30, moisture 55–65%, pH 7.5–8.5, EC <4 mS cm⁻¹ when optimizing gas mitigation (DOI).
  5. Cure to maturity before farm sale or field use — immature compost pathogen/weed risk (NRCS FAQ maturity caution; Compost Blend Safety).
  6. Document batch IDs, % biochar (state volume vs weight), feedstock, and lab sheets for both CPS 336 and any CDR offtake (Matrix Pathways for Credits).

ABI draft economic narrative (qualitative): operators report shorter processing, fewer turns (fuel/labor), and higher nutrient value product (PDF). Rexius case in that draft: 5% by volume windrow comparison with higher moisture/nitrates/nutrients vs control pile.

Manure as inoculant (paid vs unpaid)

NRCS allows mixing biochar with raw manure as inoculant; CPS 336 does not pay for the manure—contract the 100% biochar scenario and mix at producer expense under a nutrient management plan; only biochar counts toward minimum quantity (FAQ). Watch P index / high-P soils when using manure-rich chars or blends.

Practice lifespan: 1 year; may be contracted up to 5 times; ranking improves when stacked with other soil-health practices (FAQ).
